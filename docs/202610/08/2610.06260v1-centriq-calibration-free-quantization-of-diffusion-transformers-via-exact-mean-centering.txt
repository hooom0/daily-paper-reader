ABSTRACT
Diffusion transformers (DiTs) achieve state-of-the-art image generation, but their
sampling cost limits deployment. Quantizing both weights and activations to 4 bits
reduces this cost, yet existing methods fall short in one of two ways. Calibration-
based methods are tied to a specific checkpoint and prompt distribution, whereas
data-free Hadamard rotation, effective for LLMs, loses quality on DiTs. We show
that this loss has a structural cause. Adaptive layer-norm conditioning adds a
per-token mean to the activations, and at the widths of the evaluated DiTs, the
Hadamard rotations used by data-free methods cannot spread this mean uniformly
across coordinates. A single dominant direction therefore survives the rotation and
sets the quantization range. We introduce CentriQ, a calibration-free quantizer that
centers each token before rotation and restores the mean exactly through a rank-1
full-precision branch, so that per-token scales follow in closed form without data.
Weights are fitted under a robust ℓp objective that tracks the dense mode of each
group and discounts heavy tails. Across three DiTs, CentriQ matches the quality
of calibrated SVDQuant at 4 bits, whereas calibration-free weight quantizers with
plain per-token activation quantization collapse or degrade substantially. CentriQ
outperforms the strongest calibration-free method reported to date at 2-bit weights.
It is also the first calibration-free method to retain usable image quality at 2-bit
activations.

INTRODUCTION
Diffusion transformers (DiTs) (Peebles & Xie, 2023) underlie state-of-the-art text-to-image models,
but sampling them is expensive. Each image requires many denoising steps, and each step is domi-
nated by the linear layers of the transformer blocks. Unlike autoregressive LLM decoding, which at
small batch sizes is limited by memory bandwidth, a DiT forward pass is compute-bound. Weight-
only quantization (e.g. W4A16) therefore reduces memory but not latency, because the low-bit
weights are upcast to 16 bits before the matrix multiply, so activations must be quantized as well.
The post-training methods that do reach W4A4 on diffusion models all rely on calibration. A
prompt set is run through the model to fit smoothing factors, clipping ranges, rotations, or error-
compensating weight updates to the activation statistics of that specific model. SVDQuant (Li et al.,
2025), for instance, migrates activation outliers into the weights using smoothing factors computed
from COCO caption prompts, absorbs the magnified weight outliers in a 16-bit low-rank branch,
and in its final version also applies GPTQ to the residual.
Calibration has costs beyond its compute budget.
It makes the quantized model depend on a
dataset, it must be repeated for every model, fine-tune, and quantization configuration, and its
quality depends on how well the calibration prompts and timesteps match the deployment distri-
bution. Calibration-free methods that reach four-bit activations on image DiTs have appeared only
recently (Sec. 2), and they evaluate on generation benchmarks that do not measure fidelity to the
full-precision model.
Two properties of DiT activations make the calibration-free setting difficult. First, adaptive layer
normalization shifts and rescales every token with conditioning-dependent parameters, so each ac-
tivation vector carries a per-token mean. A normalized Hadamard rotation, the standard tool for

spreading outliers, maps this component onto one or a few coordinates, and the resulting outlier sets
the quantization range for the whole vector. Second, only regular Hadamard matrices, whose rows
have equal sums, spread this component uniformly, and these exist only at orders n = 4u2. None
of the hidden widths of PixArt-Σ (1152), FLUX.1 (3072), and SANA-1.6B (2240) has this form,
so rotation methods operate through block-structured or Kronecker constructions, which inherit the
mean problem.
We present CentriQ, a calibration-free W4A4 post-training quantization method for DiTs that ad-
dresses both properties directly. It subtracts the per-token mean before rotation, quantizes only the
residual, and restores the mean exactly as a rank-1 term in the layer output, so the quantization
range no longer depends on the offset. The centered activation is rotated by a deterministic fac-
torized Hadamard matrix, and weights are quantized to grouped INT4 under a robust ℓp (p < 1)
objective, so neither path uses calibration data.
Our contributions can be summarized as follows:
• We show that adaLN places a per-token mean in DiT activations, which a Hadamard ro-
tation concentrates onto one or a few coordinates that set the quantization range (Figs. 1
and 2).
• We show that after centering, the rotated operand, its per-token scale, and its quantization
error contain no term that depends on the row sums of the rotation (Sec. 4.2, App. C.5).
The choice of rotation is thereby decoupled from the mean, so CentriQ mixes globally with
factorized Hadamard matrices and restores the mean exactly as a rank-1 term in the GEMM
epilogue.
• CentriQ uses no calibration data and reaches FID and ImageReward that match calibration-
based methods at W4A4 on three DiT families. Furthermore, it surpasses the strongest
reported calibration-free method at W2A4 and is the first calibration-free method to hold
image quality at W4A2 (Tab. 1, Tab. 4, Tab. 5).

RELATED WORK
PTQ for diffusion models.
Work on U-Net diffusion models showed that activation statistics
drift with the timestep (Li et al., 2023; Shang et al., 2023; He et al., 2023; So et al., 2023; Huang
et al., 2024). Methods for diffusion transformers inherit this diagnosis and absorb the drift with
calibration: PTQ4DiT (Wu et al., 2024) weights salient channels by timestep, Q-DiT (Chen et al.,
2025) and ViDiT-Q (Zhao et al., 2025) use dynamic per-timestep or per-token activation scales, Di-
TAS (Dong & Zhang, 2025) aggregates smoothing factors across timesteps, LRQ-DiT (Yang et al.,
2025) chooses rotations per layer from a calibrated metric, and AdaTSQ (Zhang et al., 2026) allo-
cates bit-widths along the trajectory with Fisher-weighted Hessian calibration. The fitted statistics
belong to one checkpoint and sampler, so these methods must be refit when the model or guidance
changes. Sampler-side corrections (He et al., 2023; Ryu et al., 2026) act after quantization and
are complementary to any quantizer, whereas training-based approaches (He et al., 2024; Sui et al.,
2024; Yang et al., 2026; Huang et al., 2026) fall outside the post-training setting.
Rotation and incoherence.
LLM quantization spreads activation outliers across channels by ro-
tating the residual stream, with random orthogonal matrices in QuIP (Chee et al., 2023), randomized
Hadamard transforms in QuIP# (Tseng et al., 2024), and Hadamard rotations folded into the weights
in QuaRot (Ashkboos et al., 2024b). Learned rotations (Liu et al., 2025; Lin et al., 2024; Sun et al.,
2025; Hu et al., 2025) recover accuracy that a fixed Hadamard loses, but they are fit on calibration
data. All of these assume a Hadamard matrix of the model width, which the Sylvester construction
gives only for powers of two, so other widths use a Kronecker product H2k ⊗Hm with a small
hand-picked Hm.
Mean removal.
SliceGPT (Ashkboos et al., 2024a) and QuaRot fold LayerNorm mean subtrac-
tion into the preceding weights, which is exact in LLMs. In a DiT, the adaLN modulation depends
on the timestep and prompt and makes the mean vary per token, so this static fold does not apply.
HadaNorm (Federici et al., 2025) subtracts a per-channel mean computed over tokens at inference

and applies SmoothQuant-style per-channel scales, fitted on a calibration set, before a Hadamard
transform, and reports W4A4 on PixArt-Σ. CentriQ removes the per-token mean instead and re-
quires no calibration data. To our knowledge, no public implementation of HadaNorm is available,
which precludes a direct comparison under our protocol. ConvRot (Huang et al., 2025) spreads the
mean with group-wise regular Hadamard transforms of order 4k, which mix only within each group,
whereas CentriQ removes the mean and mixes globally with factorized Hadamard matrices.
Calibrated W4A4 for DiTs.
SVDQuant (Li et al., 2025) was the first to reach reasonable 4-bit
weights and activations on image DiTs, by keeping a low-rank branch in 16 bits after SmoothQuant-
style migration (Xiao et al., 2023) fit on captioned images, with fused kernels in Nunchaku.
DiRotQ (Sharify et al., 2026) preserves a calibration-derived PCA subspace of the activations at
higher precision and quantizes weights with GPTQ (Frantar et al., 2023), improving over SVDQuant
on PixArt-Σ under the MJHQ-30K protocol, which is not directly comparable with the MJHQ-5k
protocol used here. HQ-DiT (Liu & Zhang, 2024) reaches 4-bit operands with a floating-point grid.
Calibration-free quantizers.
On the weight side, RTN, NF4 (Dettmers et al., 2023), HQQ (Badri
& Shaji, 2023), and SINQ (M¨uller et al., 2026) operate on the weight tensor alone. NF4 assumes
near-Gaussian weights, HQQ fits the dense mode under an ℓp objective with p < 1, and SINQ
balances a second scale axis with Sinkhorn iterations. Calibration-free 4-bit activations on DiTs are
recent. DVD-Quant (Li et al., 2026) achieves W4A4 for video DiTs with grid refinement, rotated
quantization, and adaptive bit switching. ConvRot achieves it on FLUX-family models with group-
wise rotation, NVFP4 weights, and per-tensor FP8 activation scales. OrbitQuant (Lee et al., 2026)
quantizes weights and activations in a randomly permuted block-Hadamard basis with a Lloyd–Max
codebook derived from the post-rotation marginal (Zandieh et al., 2026), and reports GenEval on
FLUX.1 and Z-Image-Turbo down to W2A4. Neither image method removes the token mean, both
mix within blocks whose order divides the width, and both evaluate on generation benchmarks that
do not measure fidelity to the full-precision model.
Position of this work.
CentriQ is calibration-free on both axes and produces integer W4A4
operands for every transformer-block projection. We evaluate it on PixArt-Σ, FLUX.1-schnell,
and SANA-1.6B with FID, ImageReward, LPIPS, and PSNR against the 16-bit reference on MJHQ,
and compare against the published results of calibrated SVDQuant. To our knowledge it is the first
calibration-free integer W4A4 method evaluated for fidelity across three image-DiT architecture
families.

RESULTS
We evaluate CentriQ on PixArt-Σ, SANA-1.6B, and FLUX.1-schnell at 10242 resolution, using
MJHQ-5k and sDCI following the SVDQuant protocol, plus GenEval on FLUX.1-schnell. All
methods are run with identical prompts, seeds, and metric code, and design choices were made
on a disjoint development split. Full details of models, samplers, metrics, baselines, and hardware
are given in Appendix A.
Tab. 1 compares W4A4 methods on PixArt-Σ, FLUX.1-schnell, and SANA-1.6B. Without cali-
bration, CentriQ matches calibrated SVDQuant: relative to each method’s 16-bit reference, FID and
ImageReward are comparable on all three models, and CentriQ attains the lowest LPIPS. On SANA-
1.6B, CentriQ stays within 0.2 FID of the BF16 model on both benchmarks. SVDQuant attains a
higher PSNR on PixArt-Σ and SANA-1.6B, whereas CentriQ attains a lower LPIPS, which weights
perceptual structure over pixel-wise intensity. Both methods reach an FID below their 16-bit ref-
erence on FLUX.1-schnell, which lies within the noise of FID at 5k samples (Chong & Forsyth,
2020).
RTN, NF4, and HQQ collapse on PixArt-Σ and degrade substantially on FLUX.1-schnell and
SANA-1.6B, so among the evaluated methods CentriQ is the only calibration-free one that pro-
duces usable images on all three models. CentriQ achieves this with the same grouped 4-bit weight
format as RTN and HQQ.
Fig. 4 compares SVDQuant and CentriQ on GenEval prompts at W4A4, W4A2, and W2A4, and
Figs. 12, 13, and 14 in the appendix compare them at W4A4 on all three models. All images in
a row share the prompt, seed, scheduler, and step count, which is the same seed-level pairing that
underlies the fidelity metrics in Tab. 1.
5.1
LATENCY RESULTS
We measure the latency of CentriQ at W4A4 on FLUX.1-schnell on a single A100-80GB GPU at
1024×1024 resolution and 4 denoising steps. The deployed model uses a single-pass activation
quantization kernel together with Nunchaku’s 4-bit GEMMs. CentriQ generates an image in 1.69 s,
whereas the BF16 model takes 1.77 s. The full calibration-free pipeline therefore runs slightly faster
than the unquantized model as the time saved by the 4-bit matrix multiplication covers the cost of
the added operations.

CONCLUSION
We showed that adaptive layer normalization places a per-token mean in DiT activations, which a
Hadamard rotation concentrates onto a few coordinates that dominate the quantization range. Cen-
triQ centers each token before rotation and restores the mean exactly through a rank-1 term, which
makes the quantization range independent of the rotation, and it quantizes weights under a robust
ℓp objective, so no step requires calibration data. At W4A4, CentriQ matches calibrated SVDQuant
on all three models, and its centering and rotation add no net latency over BF16 inference. On
FLUX.1-schnell, it also retains usable image quality at W2A4 and extends calibration-free quanti-
zation to 2-bit activations. A fused kernel for 2-bit activations and an extension to video diffusion
transformers are natural next steps.

EXPERIMENTAL SETUP
Models.
We evaluate CentriQ on PixArt-Σ, SANA-1.6B and FLUX.1-schnell. The three models
use different attention designs (full self-attention, linear attention, and joint text-image attention
in an MM-DiT), and each injects conditioning through adaLN shift terms, which produce the per-
token mean that centering removes. All three generate 10242 images: PixArt-Σ and SANA-1.6B
sample with 20 steps of DPM-Solver and Flow-DPM-Solver, respectively, at a guidance scale of 4.5,
whereas FLUX.1-schnell samples with 4 flow-matching Euler steps at a guidance scale of 0.
Benchmarks.
Following the SVDQuant protocol, we generate one image per prompt for the 5,000
prompts of MJHQ-5k and the 5,000 prompts of sDCI. Each prompt has a fixed seed that all methods
share, so the paired metrics compare outputs generated from the same initial noise. On FLUX.1-
schnell, we additionally report GenEval, which scores compositional prompts using an object detec-
tor on 553 prompts with four images per prompt. All configuration choices, including the rotation
layout and the clip percentile, were made on a disjoint 1,000-prompt development split of MJHQ.
The evaluation split was scored once per final configuration, so no reported number informed a
design decision.
Metrics.
FID and ImageReward measure image quality, with FID computed against the bench-
mark’s reference images. PSNR and LPIPS measure fidelity to the 16-bit model by comparing
each quantized output with the 16-bit output for the same prompt and seed. Both are computed on
decoded 8-bit RGB images, and PSNR uses a peak value of 255.
Baselines.
We run RTN, NF4, and HQQ in our harness with identical prompts, seeds, and metric
code, and in their rows of Tab. 1 activations are quantized per token without a transform. For Tab. 1,
we report SVDQuant from Li et al. (2025), whose MJHQ-5k and sDCI protocol we follow. For
GenEval and the qualitative figures, we run SVDQuant from the Nunchaku wheel with smoothing
factors calibrated on COCO captions, as in the original work.

Hardware and kernels.
All generation and latency measurements run on a single NVIDIA A100-

REPRODUCIBILITY STATEMENT
The paper specifies every component needed to reimplement CentriQ. Sec. 4.2 and App. B.1 define
the centering, the factorized Hadamard rotation, the padding rule, and the closed-form per-token
scale, and Sec. 4.3 and App. B.3 give the ℓp objective, the solver, and all hyperparameters, which
are shared across models and layers (Tab. 3). App. B.2 describes how the rank-1 term and the zero-
point correction are fused into the Nunchaku 4-bit GEMM. All models, prompt sets (MJHQ-5k,
sDCI, GenEval), and metrics are public, and App. A lists the samplers, step counts, guidance scales,
seeding protocol, and the Nunchaku release used for SVDQuant.
ETHICS STATEMENT
CentriQ lowers the cost of running text-to-image models, which widens access for legitimate uses
and for misuse alike. The quantized models inherit the safety behavior and biases of their 16-
bit sources, and this work does not evaluate either. We collected no new data and ran no studies
with human participants. All prompt sets come from public benchmarks and are used under their
respective licenses.
