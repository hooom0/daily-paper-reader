## ABSTRACT 

Text-to-image diffusion models can generate prohibited content, which motivates concept erasure through machine unlearning. Most erasure methods intervene at the text interface, through prompt modification or localized updates to text-conditioning weights, and they are evaluated by what the model outputs for given prompts. Such evaluation cannot see what the network still encodes. Latent-space auditing, which bypasses text conditioning and probes the denoising network directly, shows that erased concepts remain recoverable from internal representations. We find that this also holds for methods built to be robust against adversarial prompts, and that the problem grows with the number of erased concepts. We propose Auditing-Aware Unlearning for Verifiable Concept Erasure in Diffusion Models (AVCE) , a framework that grounds erasure in the model’s latent representations. AVCE audits the embedding neighborhood of each concept and condenses the discovered vulnerable directions into an anchor at the weakest geometric point. It edits cross-attention and self-attention projections in closed form at this anchor, then fine-tunes the two pathways with pathway-level auditing losses, using orthogonal gradient projection to consolidate multiple concepts. Experiments on SD v1.5, SDXL, and Flux 1.0 across object, explicit-content, and artistic-style unlearning show that AVCE reduces attack success rates by 5.07 × and improves auditing scores by 3.84 × over the strongest baseline, while preserving competitive generation quality. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Text-to-image (T2I) diffusion models generate diverse, high-fidelity images from natural language descriptions (Rombach et al., 2022; Ma et al., 2024; Podell et al., 2023; Saharia et al., 2022; Nichol et al., 2021; Ding et al., 2022; Zhou et al., 2022). Their wide deployment also raises ethical and legal risks, including copyright infringement, unauthorized trademark generation, and the synthesis of malicious imagery (Kazerouni et al., 2022; Xing et al., 2024; Jang et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2024; Nguyen et al., 2022; Weidinger et al., 2021; Brundage et al., 2018; Tolosana et al., 2020; Solaiman & Dennison, 2021). Machine unlearning (MU) addresses these risks by removing specific knowledge from a trained model without retraining it from scratch (Bourtoule et al., 2021; Guo et al., 2019; Graves et al., 2021; Xu et al., 2024; Marino et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2023; Tu et al., 2025; Feng et al., 2025). In T2I generation, MU takes the form of concept erasure: the mapping between text prompts and a target visual concept is severed so that the model no longer renders the concept. Existing methods erase individual objects, styles, or identities (Jia et al., 2023; Kumari et al., 2023; Chavhan et al., 2024; Gandikota et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2024a; Schramowski et al., 2023; Fan et al., 2023; Heng & Soh, 2023), and recent work extends erasure to multiple concepts at once (Lu et al., 2024; Lyu et al., 2024; Li et al., 2025b; Gandikota et al., 2024; Gong et al., 2024; Deng et al., 2026). 1arXiv:2610.05401v1 [cs.LG] 4 Oct 2026 Preprint Whether an erased concept has actually been removed is usually judged from model outputs. Bench-marks (Moon et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2024b) score the images generated for a set of prompts, and adversarial evaluations (Zhang et al., 2024d; Chin et al., 2023; Pham et al., 2023; Tsai et al., 2023) search for prompts or embeddings that make the concept reappear. In response to these attacks, robust erasure methods harden the text-to-image mapping through adversarial training (Kim et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024c), erasure guided by inverted worst-case embeddings (Li et al., 2026; Srivatsan et al., 2025), or closed-form re-erasure of adversarial embeddings (Gong et al., 2024). These defenses bring attack success rates below those of non-robust baselines (Fig. 1, left). Low attack success, however, does not establish that the concept is gone. The latent-space audit of Sharma et al. (2024) bypasses text conditioning altogether: it injects a concept-informative latent code at an intermediate denoising step (partial diffusion) and measures, through the Concept Retrieval Score (CRS) and the Concept Confidence Score (CCS), whether the unlearned model still reconstructs the concept (Appendix A.2). Under this audit, methods that block adversarial prompts still retain the concept in their internal representations (Fig. 1, middle and right; Section 4). The cause is structural. These methods act mainly on the text encoder or on the cross-attention projections: the key and value projections of the cross-attention layers hold 2.23% of the UNet parameters in SD v1.5 and 13.27% in SDXL. Such edits sever the pathway from text to concept and leave intact the capacity of the denoising network to reconstruct concept-related patterns, which is the capacity that partial diffusion probes. Attack robustness therefore shows only that the evaluated attacks fail to recover the erased concept; it does not establish that concept information has been removed from the learned representations. The discrepancy widens in multi-concept unlearning, where existing methods degrade as the number of erased concepts grows. We therefore design erasure around the audit rather than around the attack surface, and propose 

Auditing-Aware Unlearning for Verifiable Concept Erasure in Diffusion Models (AVCE) , a framework that removes a concept from both the text-conditioning pathway and the visual pathway of the denoising network. AVCE proceeds in three stages. It first searches the embedding neighborhood of each concept for directions along which the concept can be revived, and condenses them into a 

Verifiable Vulnerability Anchor located at the weakest point of that neighborhood. It then performs 

Analytical Editing at this anchor: a closed-form rewrite of the cross-attention projections severs the association between text and concept, and a closed-form shrinkage of the self-attention projections suppresses the residual visual patterns of the concept. Finally, Audit-Guided Refinement fine-tunes the two pathways in alternation with pathway-level auditing losses that remove the non-linear leakage left by the linear edits, and applies orthogonal gradient projection so that multiple concepts can be erased within one model. Our contributions are as follows: 

• The Illusion of Robustness. We apply latent-space auditing to robust concept erasure methods and show that blocking adversarial prompts does not remove the concept: once text conditioning is bypassed, its visual features remain recoverable from the internal representations of the model. 

• Auditing-Aware Verifiable Concept Erasure. We propose AVCE, which moves erasure from the text-conditioning interface to the internal representations of the model. AVCE combines a Verifiable Vulnerability Anchor obtained by auditing the embedding space, closed-form editing of both cross-attention and self-attention projections, and fine-tuning with pathway-level auditing losses. 

• Scalable Multi-Concept Erasure. AVCE handles inter-concept interference with difficulty-aware sampling and orthogonal gradient projection. On SD v1.5, SDXL, and Flux 1.0, AVCE reduces attack success rates by 5.07 × and improves auditing scores by 3.84 × over the strongest baseline.

Training-free methods such as ConceptPrune (Chavhan et al., 2024) identify and prune the neurons responsible for a concept without further optimization. Guidance-based methods such as SLD (Schramowski et al., 2023) steer the sampling trajectory away from undesired content and leave the pretrained weights unchanged. Multi-concept erasure follows three directions. Fine-tuning approaches, including AGE (Bui et al., 2025), CoE (Li et al., 2025a), and Sculpting Memory (Li et al., 2025b), iteratively optimize model weights or masks to balance forgetting and preservation. Analytical methods such as UCE (Gandikota et al., 2024) and ScaPre (Deng et al., 2026) modify weight matrices in closed form. Adapter-based methods, including MACE (Lu et al., 2024), SPM (Lyu et al., 2024), and GLOCE (Lee et al., 2025), attach lightweight modules such as LoRAs or one-dimensional adapters that suppress target concepts while the backbone remains fixed. 

Robust unlearning. AdvUnlearn (Zhang et al., 2024c) integrates adversarial training into concept era-sure to counter prompt attacks while preserving utility. RACE (Kim et al., 2024) mitigates adversarial text embeddings with an efficient single-timestep adversarial training scheme. STEREO (Srivatsan et al., 2025) first identifies vulnerable embeddings and then performs anchor-guided erasure to resist embedding-space inversion. RECE (Gong et al., 2024) iterates closed-form solutions for rapid and robust alignment without extensive fine-tuning. AEGIS (Li et al., 2026) addresses the trade-off between robustness and retention in data-free settings through target-guided erasure and gradient synergy. AVCE differs from these methods in where it intervenes: it edits self-attention projections in addition to cross-attention projections, and it uses pathway-level auditing signals during fine-tuning. 

Evaluating concept erasure. Erasure is evaluated at three levels. Output-level benchmarks (Moon et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2024b) score the images generated for fixed prompt sets with concept-specific classifiers. Attack-level evaluations search the text interface for inputs that revive the concept, through genetic search over discrete tokens (Tsai et al., 2023), optimization of token perturbations against the diffusion objective (Zhang et al., 2024d; Chin et al., 2023), or direct inversion of the erased embedding (Pham et al., 2023). Both levels observe the model only through its text interface. Representation-level auditing instead bypasses that interface: the partial-diffusion protocol of Sharma et al. (2024) injects a concept-informative latent at an intermediate denoising step and measures, through CRS and CCS, whether the unlearned network still reconstructs the concept. We adopt this protocol as our primary evaluation and, unlike prior work, also use its signal inside the erasure procedure.

Existing unlearning methods apply erasure at the original prompt embedding and leave robustness to separate, post-hoc defenses. The neighborhood of that embedding, however, may contain directions along which the erased concept can be revived. We therefore audit this neighborhood before editing and replace the original embedding with an anchor that summarizes the vulnerable directions found. For a set of N target concepts {C i}Ni=1 , let co,i ∈ Rdin be the original text embedding of the i-th concept. We optimize probe embeddings ca,i that reproduce the generative behavior of Ci while lying far from co,i . A search through the full denoising network alone tends to return embeddings that revive the concept by re-routing the attention maps of the cross-attention (CA) layers, that is, through prompt alignment, rather than through the visual content that the CA layers inject and the self-attention (SA) layers propagate. ## 4 EXPERIMENT 

We evaluate AVCE on three concept unlearning tasks: multi-concept object unlearning (Section 4.2), explicit-content unlearning (Section 4.3), and artistic-style unlearning (Section 4.4), all under the protocol of Section 4.1. Section 4.5 presents ablation studies. Appendix B reports results on additional backbones (Tables 8, 9, 10), large-scale unlearning (Fig. 6), and runtime (Table 7). 4.1 EXPERIMENTAL SETTINGS 

All experiments are primarily conducted on Stable Diffusion XL (SDXL) (Podell et al., 2023), with additional evaluations on Stable Diffusion v1.5 (SD v1.5) (Rombach et al., 2022) and Flux 1.0 (Labs, 2024). We compare AVCE against eight representative baselines: ESD (Gandikota et al., 2023), SPM (Lyu et al., 2024), MACE (Lu et al., 2024), SM (Li et al., 2025b), UCE (Gandikota et al., 2024), RECE (Gong et al., 2024), STEREO (Srivatsan et al., 2025), and AdvUnlearn (AdvU) (Zhang et al., 2024c). Multi-object unlearning is evaluated on Imagenette (Howard et al., 2019), a ten-class ImageNet subset. Explicit-content unlearning uses the I2P dataset (Schramowski et al., 2023). For artistic-style unlearning, we target five styles and follow prior settings (Chavhan et al., 2024; Deng et al., 2026). To evaluate attack success rate (ASR), we use concept-specific classifiers to detect whether erased concepts reappear in generated images: an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet50 for objects (Chavhan et al., 2024), NudeNet for explicit content (Bedapudi, 2019), and a ViT-base model fine-tuned on WikiArt for styles (Zhang et al., 2024d). We further assess robustness using Ring-A-Bell (Tsai et al., 2023) (ASR1), UnlearnDiffAtk (Zhang et al., 2024d) (ASR2), and P4D (Chin et al., 2023) (ASR3). We also adopt Concept Confidence Score (CCS) and Concept Retrieval Score (CRS) (Sharma et al., 2024) as auditing metrics to distinguish genuine erasure from latent-space concealment. We evaluate FID and CLIP score on COCO-30K, a 30K-caption subset of MS-COCO (Lin et al., 2014), to measure generative fidelity and text-image alignment after unlearning. AVCE is trained and evaluated on NVIDIA H100 (80 GB) GPUs. For reproducibility, all baseline results are obtained from official open-source implementations (Appendix C). 4.2 OBJECT UNLEARNING

## 5 CONCLUSION 

Robust concept erasure methods block adversarial prompts, yet latent-space auditing shows that the erased concept remains in the internal representations of the diffusion model. AVCE moves erasure from the text-conditioning interface to these representations. It audits the embedding neighborhood of each concept to build a Verifiable Vulnerability Anchor at the weakest geometric point, edits cross-attention and self-attention projections in closed form at this anchor, and then closes non-linear leakage with Audit-Guided Refinement, using orthogonal gradient projection to consolidate multiple concepts. Across three diffusion architectures and three unlearning tasks, AVCE lowers attack success rates and improves auditing scores over existing baselines while keeping generation quality competitive. These results indicate that erasure should be assessed and optimized at the level of what the model encodes, rather than only what it outputs for given prompts. What such an assessment can establish is bounded by the audit itself: our conclusions rest on one auditing protocol and three prompt-level attacks, and Appendix D states the assumptions this leaves open. 

## ETHICS STATEMENT 

This work studies the removal of prohibited concepts, including explicit content, from text-to-image diffusion models, with the goal of safer model deployment. Explicit-content experiments use the public I2P prompt benchmark and the NudeNet detector solely to measure how much unsafe content remains after erasure. The adversarial attacks (Ring-A-Bell, UnlearnDiffAtk, P4D) and the latent-space auditing protocol are existing public methods, and we use them only to evaluate erased models. The study involves no human subjects and collects no new data.

## A DETAILED SETTINGS 

This appendix provides implementation details: the adversarial attack configurations used for ro-bustness evaluation (Appendix A.1), the latent-space auditing protocol (Appendix A.2), the dataset construction for all tasks (Appendix A.3), and the parameter settings of AVCE (Appendix A.4). A.1 ADVERSARIAL ATTACK SETTINGS 

We evaluate robustness against three representative adversarial attacks spanning both white-box and black-box settings. 

Ring-A-Bell (ASR1) (Tsai et al., 2023). Ring-A-Bell is a black-box, model-agnostic attack that constructs adversarial prompts without direct access to the target model’s parameters. It first performs concept extraction by computing the mean embedding difference between prompts containing and lacking the target concept, forming an empirical concept representation. A genetic algorithm then optimizes discrete prompt tokens to maximize alignment with this representation. Following prior work (Zhang et al., 2024c; Srivatsan et al., 2025; Li et al., 2026), we set the weight of empirical concept to 3 and the prompt length to 16. For each evaluated model, one image is generated per adversarial prompt. 

UnlearnDiffAtk (ASR2) (Zhang et al., 2024d). UnlearnDiffAtk is a white-box attack that exploits the intrinsic classification capability of diffusion models to craft adversarial prompts. It optimizes discrete token perturbations via projection onto the probability simplex, without requiring an auxiliary reference model. Following the established protocol (Zhang et al., 2024c; Srivatsan et al., 2025; Gong et al., 2024; Li et al., 2026), we set the number of modifiable tokens to k=5 for explicit-content unlearning and k=3 for object and artistic-style unlearning. The perturbations are optimized across 50 diffusion timesteps for 40 iterations, using the AdamW optimizer with a learning rate of 0.01. 

P4D (ASR3) (Chin et al., 2023). Prompting4Debugging (P4D) is a white-box attack that optimizes adversarial prompts in the continuous embedding space and projects them back onto discrete tokens. It enforces similarity between noise predictions of the pre-trained and concept-erased models, effec-tively searching for prompts that bypass the erasure defense. We adopt the same configuration as UnlearnDiffAtk: k=5 modifiable tokens for explicit-content unlearning and k=3 for the other tasks, with 50 diffusion timesteps and 40 optimization iterations using AdamW at a learning rate of 0.01. 

Attack-Specific Classifiers. To determine attack success, we use the same concept-specific clas-sifiers described in the main text (Section 4.1). For object unlearning, an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet50 (Chavhan et al., 2024) classifies generated images. For explicit content, NudeNet (Beda-pudi, 2019) detects unsafe body-part categories; an image is deemed to contain inappropriate con-tent if any of the following labels are detected: ANUS_EXPOSED , FEMALE_BREAST_EXPOSED ,

FEMALE_GENITALIA_EXPOSED , or MALE_GENITALIA_EXPOSED . For artistic-style unlearn-ing, a ViT-base model fine-tuned on WikiArt (Zhang et al., 2024d) is used, with attack success determined by the top-1 prediction. A.2 LATENT -S PACE AUDITING SETTINGS 

Beyond output-level adversarial evaluation, we employ the latent-space auditing framework proposed by Sharma et al. (2024) to assess whether unlearning methods achieve genuine concept erasure or merely suppress surface-level generation. 

Partial Diffusion Protocol. The core of the auditing framework is a partial diffusion pipeline that bypasses the text-conditioning pathway entirely. Given a fully trained model θ and an unlearned model θ∗, the procedure operates as follows: (1) A reference image xref of the target concept is generated using the fully trained model θ with the concept prompt P. (2) The reference image is encoded into the latent space and noise is injected up to an intermediate timestep t = ψ · T ,where ψ ∈ (0 , 1) is the partial diffusion ratio and T is the total number of timesteps. (3) The unlearned model θ∗ then denoises this intermediate latent from timestep t back to t=0 , using a specified guidance scale η and an empty prompt (i.e., no text conditioning). This procedure injects 16 Preprint concept-informative latent codes at intermediate denoising steps, probing whether the unlearned model’s deep parameters still retain the ability to reconstruct concept-related visual patterns. 

Evaluation Metrics. Two complementary metrics are computed from the partial diffusion outputs: • Concept Retrieval Score (CRS) measures the feature-level similarity between partially diffused outputs and the original concept domain. Specifically, CRS computes the cosine similarity in CLIP feature space between the output of the partial diffusion pipeline and the reference image. A higher CRS indicates that the concept’s visual features are more thoroughly removed from the model’s internal representations, meaning the unlearned model fails to reconstruct concept-related patterns even when provided with concept-informative latents. For genuinely unlearned models, CRS should be high (close to 1.0), reflecting that the model no longer encodes the target concept. • Concept Confidence Score (CCS) quantifies the confidence of a domain classifier in assigning the partially diffused output to the pre-unlearning concept domain. A lower CCS indicates that the model’s internal representations no longer encode concept-discriminative features. For genuinely unlearned models, CCS should be low (close to 0.0). 

Hyperparameters. Following the default settings in Sharma et al. (2024), we set the partial diffusion ratio to ψ = 0 .6 and evaluate across a sweep of guidance scales η ∈ { 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 }. We report the CCS and CRS values averaged over the guidance sweep to ensure robustness of the evaluation. For each target concept, 50 reference images are generated from the fully trained model using distinct random seeds, and the metrics are averaged across all reference images. The DDIM scheduler with 50 inference steps is used for both the forward noise injection and the reverse denoising process. A.3 DATASET CONSTRUCTION 

We describe the datasets used for the three unlearning tasks, together with the extended benchmark for large-scale multi-concept evaluation. 

Object Unlearning. We evaluate multi-concept object unlearning on Imagenette (Howard et al., 2019), a ten-class subset of ImageNet containing easily recognizable categories: tench , En-glish springer , cassette player , chain saw , church , French horn , garbage truck , gas pump ,

golf ball , and parachute . For each target class, we construct evaluation prompts using 

"a photo of a [class name]" and several variants such as "[class name] in a natural setting" and "a realistic image of [class name]" . Following prior works (Gandikota et al., 2023; Chavhan et al., 2024; Deng et al., 2026), we generate 500 images per class with different random seeds to measure Unlearn Acc. Unlearn Acc is the top-1 accuracy of an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet50 on the images generated for the erased classes. 

Explicit Content Unlearning. We evaluate explicit-content unlearning on the I2P dataset (Schramowski et al., 2023), which contains prompts likely to induce unsafe generations, including nudity, violence, self-harm, and shocking imagery. Following prior works (Zhang et al., 2024c; Srivatsan et al., 2025; Gong et al., 2024), we focus on erasing nudity as a representative sensitive concept. For each model, we generate one image per prompt, yielding 4,703 images in total. NudeNet is applied with a threshold of 0.6 under the default I2P setting. Generation quality is further evaluated on COCO-30K (Lin et al., 2014) using FID and CLIP score. 

Artistic-Style Unlearning. For artistic-style unlearning, we follow the protocol of Chavhan et al. (2024); Deng et al. (2026); Gandikota et al. (2023) and erase five styles: Van Gogh , Pablo Pi-casso , Rembrandt , Andy Warhol , and Claude Monet . For each artist, 20 prompts are used to gen-erate evaluation images, such as "A painting in the style of [artist name]" and 

"[subject] by [artist name]" . We report three CLIP-based metrics: CLIP a for align-ment with the erased style, CLIP c for generation quality on MS-COCO, and CLIP d = CLIP c −CLIP a

as a measure of erasure selectivity averaged over the five target styles. 

Extended Benchmark for Large-Scale Unlearning. To assess scalability beyond standard bench-marks, we additionally use the extended dataset of Deng et al. (2026), which expands the forgetting scope to 5, 10, 15, and 20 concepts from a broader pool of ImageNet classes and artistic styles. Each scale includes standardized prompts, evaluation splits, and concept-specific classifiers, enabling 17 Preprint controlled comparison as the number of erased concepts increases. We use this benchmark for the scalability study in Appendix B.1 (Fig. 6), which reports Unlearn Acc, ASR, CCS, and CRS against the number of forgotten concepts. A.4 DETAILED SETTINGS OF AVCE 

Verifiable Vulnerability Anchor. For each target concept, we run K=10 randomly initialized probe searches. Each search is optimized for 200 iterations with step size β = 5 × 10 −3 using the Adam optimizer. The repulsion coefficient is set to τ = 0 .5, and the SA auditing strength to μ = 0 .3. Probe embeddings are initialized by adding Gaussian noise N (0 , 0.12I) to the original concept embedding 

co,i . Timesteps t are sampled uniformly from {1, . . . , 1000 } at each iteration, and noisy latents zt are constructed from a fixed set of 5 reference latent codes. After the search, each probe is tested over 4 fresh draws of (zt, t ): it is kept as successful if its noise-prediction error against the original-concept prediction is below half of the error obtained with the null prompt on the same draws; if no probe passes, the single best probe is kept. The principal direction ui, 1 is extracted via truncated SVD on the residual matrix ∆i formed from the successful perturbations. 

Cross-Attention Analytical Erasure. The Semantic Modulator uses P =50 calibration samples per concept, generated from the base model using diverse prompts. The Fisher stability score and Symmetric Uncertainty are computed with a temperature of σtemp = 5 .0 for the sigmoid mappings. The ridge coefficient is set to λ = 0 .1 for the closed-form solution W ⋆ = Q1Q−12 . The preservation set R contains the content-token and end-of-text embeddings of 50 neutral prompts describing everyday objects and scenes. The entire cross-attention editing is performed as a single one-shot matrix computation. 

Self-Attention Analytical Shrinkage. The Visual Localizer extracts concept-specific features using a mid-process noisy latent at timestep t=500 . The shrinkage coefficient is set to κ = 1 .0. Shrinkage is applied to all SA key and value projection matrices across all UNet layers simultaneously. 

Audit-Guided Refinement. The refinement phase alternates between CA and SA updates for 500 total iterations. We use the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 1 × 10 −5 and batch size of 1. The pathway-specific loss coefficients are set to λca = 0 .5 and λsa = 0 .5, and the retain coefficient to 

λr = 10 ; each update draws one neutral prompt at random. Timesteps are uniformly sampled at each iteration. For multi-concept consolidation, we use an EMA decay of 0.99 for the difficulty scores ˜Li

and sample B=4 concepts per mini-batch. Pairwise gradient conflicts are resolved via orthogonal projection following Eq. (14). 

## B MORE EXPERIMENTAL RESULTS 

B.1 SCALABILITY OF AVCE To evaluate scalability, we run object unlearning with an increasing number of forgotten concepts (N ∈ { 5, 10 , 15 , 20 }). Figure 6 shows that the baselines degrade as N grows: Unlearn Acc, Attack Success Rate (ASR), and Concept Confidence Score (CCS) rise, and Concept Retrieval Score (CRS) falls, which indicates interference between concepts and a loss of robust erasure at scale. AVCE degrades far more slowly. With the erasure grounded in the Verifiable Vulnerability Anchor and gradient conflicts resolved during multi-concept consolidation, it maintains low Unlearn Acc, ASR, and CCS and a high CRS even when 20 objects are erased simultaneously. Computational overhead is a further concern in multi-concept unlearning. Sequential methods incur a training time that grows linearly with the number of target concepts ( O(N )). AVCE instead updates the model for all concepts concurrently through the consolidation strategy of Section 3.3. ## D LIMITATIONS 

Our audit relies on a single protocol, the partial-diffusion audit of Sharma et al. (2024) with its CRS and CCS metrics; other probes of internal representations may expose traces that this protocol does not. Robustness is measured against three prompt-level attacks (Ring-A-Bell, UnlearnDiffAtk, and P4D). We do not evaluate attacks that adapt to the anchor construction, nor recovery of an erased concept by fine-tuning the edited model. The analytical edits act on attention projections and assume white-box access to the model weights. 21
