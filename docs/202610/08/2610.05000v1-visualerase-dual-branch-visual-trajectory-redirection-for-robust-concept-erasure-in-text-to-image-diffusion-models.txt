ABSTRACT
Concept erasure is essential for the safe deployment of text-to-image diffusion
models, as they may reproduce harmful, copyrighted, or privacy-sensitive content
learned from unconstrained large-scale data. Existing methods typically erase
unwanted concepts while preserving general generation capability by redirect-
ing target-related text-to-image mappings.
However, recent studies show that
erased models may still retain visual generative trajectories of target concepts,
leaving them vulnerable to adversarial recovery attacks and revealing a funda-
mental gap between redirecting text-to-image mappings and truly removing vi-
sual knowledge. To bridge this gap, we propose VISUALERASE, a new paradigm
that redirects concept-bearing visual generative trajectories toward explicitly de-
fined concept-removed outcomes. To enable this redirection, we use structure-
preserving image editing to construct content-aligned, concept-removed coun-
terparts for source images, providing explicit visual endpoints that retain non-
target content. We then derive a denoising target from each source-to-counterpart
pair and use a dual-branch redirection loss to align both text-conditioned and un-
conditional predictions with this target, since conditional supervision alone does
not explicitly constrain generation without textual guidance. To mitigate the ad-
verse effects of concept erasure on non-target generation, we jointly optimize
the redirection loss with a counterpart retention loss that matches denoising pre-
dictions from the frozen pretrained model. Across style, celebrity, and nudity
erasure, VISUALERASE limits the maximum attack success rate over seven at-
tacks to 0%, 8%, and 0.1%, respectively, while retaining general generation qual-
ity. These results highlight the importance of visual trajectory redirection for ro-
bust concept erasure beyond text-to-image mappings alone. The project page is
https://qianlong0502.github.io/VisualErase-Homepage/.

INTRODUCTION
Text-to-image diffusion models can synthesize diverse, high-quality images from natural-language
descriptions (Rombach et al., 2022; Nichol et al., 2021; Saharia et al., 2022). Their capabilities
emerge from training on web-scale image-text corpora, for which exhaustive content filtering re-
mains difficult (Schuhmann et al., 2022). Consequently, pretrained models can reproduce copy-
righted artistic styles, recognizable identities, and explicit content (Jiang et al., 2023; Carlini et al.,
2023; Somepalli et al., 2023; Schramowski et al., 2023). Concept erasure aims to address these
risks by removing a designated concept from a pretrained model while preserving generation of
non-target content. (Gandikota et al., 2023; Kumari et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2024a).
As illustrated in Figure 1(a), many existing methods formulate concept erasure as redirecting gen-
eration under target-related text conditions toward alternative concepts. Early methods weaken the
response to a target prompt or modify the cross-attention layers that bind text to visual features
(Gandikota et al., 2023; Kumari et al., 2023). Later methods improve scale, efficiency, or robust-
ness by editing additional textual associations and by training against adversarial prompts (Lu et al.,
2024; Lyu et al., 2024; Gong et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024b; Srivatsan et al., 2025). Prompt-based

This raises a more fundamental question: does resistance to textual reactivation imply that the un-
derlying visual generation capability has been removed? TINA+ probes this gap through text-free
visual inversion, showing that target concepts can remain recoverable despite strong resistance to
prompt-based attacks (Xiang et al., 2026a;b). Robust erasure should therefore prevent target-concept
recovery through visual generation trajectories, rather than merely suppress textual reactivation.
To address this gap, we introduce VISUALERASE, a new concept-erasure paradigm based on visual
trajectory redirection. As illustrated in Figure 1(b), its central idea is to redirect concept-bearing
denoising trajectories toward content-aligned, concept-removed counterparts that serve as explicit
visual endpoints. To construct these endpoints, we use structure-preserving image editing to form
source-to-counterpart pairs. In the evaluated style, celebrity, and nudity settings, the edits produce
realistic renderings, replace the target identity, and add scene-appropriate clothing, respectively,
while preserving non-target content, structure, and composition. We then analytically derive a de-
noising target from each source-to-counterpart pair, translating the specified visual outcome into di-
rect supervision for trajectory redirection. Conditional supervision alone, however, does not explic-
itly constrain generation without textual guidance. We therefore introduce Dual-Branch Redirection,
which aligns text-conditioned and unconditional denoising predictions with the same counterpart-
derived target. The conditional branch supervises generation under the source prompt, while the
unconditional branch extends this supervision to the empty condition. Moreover, optimizing the
erasure objective can inadvertently degrade non-target generation. To mitigate this effect, we intro-
duce Counterpart Retention, which reuses the concept-removed counterparts as retention examples.
Its loss matches denoising predictions from the frozen pretrained model on these examples and is
jointly optimized with the dual-branch redirection loss.
Extensive experiments across style, celebrity, and nudity erasure demonstrate that VISUALERASE
suppresses both standard and adversarial concept recovery while preserving general generation qual-
ity. In style erasure, it eliminates standard target recovery and achieves a 0% adversarial attack
success rate across all seven attacks, including the text-free TINA+ probe, while retaining genera-
tion quality close to the original model. VISUALERASE is also effective for celebrity and nudity
erasure, where target recovery remains zero or near zero under diverse attacks. Recent robust era-
sure methods improve resistance by alternating adversarial token search with additional erasure up-
dates (Srivatsan et al., 2025). In contrast, VISUALERASE requires neither adversarial prompt search
nor attack-generated supervision. By directly redirecting concept-bearing visual trajectories, it sup-

RELATED WORK
Concept erasure in diffusion models.
Concept erasure aims to remove undesired concepts from
pretrained diffusion models while preserving their ability to generate non-target content. Early meth-
ods fine-tune selected U-Net parameters or suppress concept-specific attention responses (Gandikota
et al., 2023; Kumari et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2024a). Subsequent work improves efficiency and
scalability through closed-form model editing or lightweight adapters (Gandikota et al., 2024; Lu
et al., 2024; Lyu et al., 2024). General machine unlearning methods further select influential param-
eters or constrain their updates when removing concepts from diffusion models (Fan et al., 2024; Wu
& Harandi, 2024; Wu et al., 2025). Despite their different parameterizations, these methods largely
follow text-to-image redirection by modifying how target-related text conditions steer generation,
without explicitly specifying the desired visual behavior after erasure.
Robust concept erasure.
The vulnerability of erased models to alternative prompts has motivated
defenses that explicitly incorporate adversarial robustness. RECE strengthens erasure against con-
cept restoration, AdvUnlearn introduces adversarial training, and STEREO alternates adversarial
token search with additional erasure updates (Gong et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024b; Srivatsan et al.,
2025). These methods improve resistance to textual circumvention, but they still build robustness
around prompts or embeddings associated with the erased concept. Our proposed VISUALERASE
instead acts directly on visual denoising trajectories without adversarial prompt mining or attack-
generated supervision.
Attacks on erased diffusion models.
Attacks on concept erasure have evolved from discrete
prompt search to continuous embedding optimization and text-free visual probes. Prompt-space
methods search for alternative expressions or optimized tokens that recover a suppressed concept
(Wen et al., 2023; Chin et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2024; Tsai et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024c). CCE in-
stead learns a continuous token embedding that serves as a proxy for the erased concept (Pham et al.,
2024). TINA+ recovers erased concepts through text-free visual inversion, revealing that concept-
related visual generation can remain accessible after textual suppression (Xiang et al., 2026b;a).
These attacks show that robust erasure must eliminate target-related visual generative trajectories
rather than only suppress particular textual triggers. This is the central objective of our proposed
VISUALERASE.

PROPOSED METHOD
Overview. VISUALERASE aims to remove a target visual concept while preserving generation of
non-target content. Existing objectives mainly redirect target-related text-to-image mappings with-
out explicitly defining the desired visual outcome after erasure. To make this objective concrete, as
shown in Figure 2, we first pair each concept-bearing source image with a content-aligned coun-
terpart in which the concept has been removed. Second, we derive a denoising target from this
source-to-counterpart transformation. Third, we design Dual-Branch Redirection to apply the target
to both conditional and unconditional generation paths. Finally, we introduce Counterpart Retention
to preserve the pretrained model’s general generation capability.

As shown in Figure 2(a), for style erasure, we use Stable Diffusion v1.5 image-to-image edit-
ing (Rombach et al., 2022) to generate a realistic full-image counterpart. We extract an HED Soft-
Edge map (Xie & Tu, 2015) and a MiDaS depth map (Ranftl et al., 2022) from the source image
and use them as complementary ControlNet conditions (Zhang et al., 2023) to preserve the scene
layout and depicted content. For celebrity erasure, we use the GIPHY Celebrity Detector (GCD)1 to
identify the face associated with the target identity. We crop a square region around its detected face
box and apply the same SoftEdge- and depth-conditioned image editor to generate a synthetic iden-
tity that does not correspond to the target celebrity while preserving pose, expression, illumination,
and surrounding appearance. We then blend the edited face through a feathered mask. For nudity
erasure, we derive garment-coverage masks from NudeNet2 detections and use the Stable Diffusion
v1.5 inpainting model3 to add scene-appropriate opaque clothing. We then restore the source pixels
outside the edited region. Across all settings, we retain only candidates that remove the target con-
cept while preserving non-target content, structure, and composition. Appendix A provides more
details.

Baselines and attacks.
We compare against concept-erasure methods with publicly available im-
plementations or task-specific checkpoints, including ESD (Gandikota et al., 2023), FMN (Zhang
et al., 2024a), AC (Kumari et al., 2023), UCE (Gandikota et al., 2024), MACE (Lu et al., 2024),
SPM (Lyu et al., 2024), RECE (Gong et al., 2024), AdvUnlearn (Zhang et al., 2024b), SalUn (Fan
et al., 2024), and STEREO (Srivatsan et al., 2025). Checkpoint availability varies across concept
categories, so each table includes the corresponding available subset.
We evaluate robustness against PEZ (Wen et al., 2023), MMA (Yang et al., 2024), RAB (Tsai et al.,
2024), P4D (Chin et al., 2024), UDA (Zhang et al., 2024c), CCE (Pham et al., 2024), and TINA+ (Xi-
ang et al., 2026a). The first six recover concepts through alternative prompts or embeddings, whereas
TINA+ uses text-free inversion to probe prompt-independent visual knowledge.
Evaluation protocol.
We report concept accuracy under standard target prompts (ACC) and at-
tack success rate (ASR), with lower values indicating stronger erasure. Following UDA, Van Gogh
recovery is measured by the Top-1 prediction of a 129-class ViT-Base style classifier pretrained on
ImageNet and fine-tuned on WikiArt (Zhang et al., 2024c; Wu et al., 2020; Saleh & Elgammal,
2015). Taylor Swift recovery is measured by exact Top-1 identity matching with GCD. For nudity,
we observe that NudeNet frequently misclassifies censored or non-explicit outputs as positive. We
therefore use a frozen VLM-based evaluator built on LlavaGuard (Helff et al., 2025) to determine
whether recognizable explicit nudity is present. Further details of this evaluation protocol and its
comparison with NudeNet are provided in Appendix B.1.
We evaluate non-target generation on COCO-30K (Lin et al., 2014). For each model, we gener-
ate 30,000 images at 512 × 512 using 50 denoising steps and a classifier-free guidance scale of
7.5 (Ho & Salimans, 2022). All models reuse the same prompts, seeds, and initial latents. We report
FID (Heusel et al., 2017) and CLIP ViT-L/14 alignment (Radford et al., 2021). CLIP values are
multiplied by 100 throughout the paper.
Implementation details.
For style and celebrity erasure, VISUALERASE uses the All-Attn scope,
updating all query, key, value, and output projections in the U-Net self-attention and cross-
attention layers, corresponding to 93.5M trainable parameters.
We optimize the model with
AdamW (Loshchilov & Hutter, 2017) using a batch size of 4, a learning rate of 2 × 10−6, and a
weight decay of 0.01 for 1,000 updates. We set λc = λu = 0.5 and λr = 1.0. For nudity, we retain
the same objective and learning rate but use a fixed Dual-Branch Redirection gradient ranking to
select the top 10% of weights within the Non-Cross scope, which comprises the U-Net linear and
convolutional parameters outside the cross-attention layers. The nudity model is trained for 5,000
updates. Its saliency construction and optimizer implementation are detailed in Appendix B.2.

Style erasure.
Table 1 reports the complete Van Gogh comparison. Under standard target prompts,

reaches 46% for STEREO, but remains at 0% for VISUALERASE. VISUALERASE also achieves

erasure must eliminate target-related visual generative trajectories rather than only alter generation
under the original target condition.

attacks.

CONCLUSION
We presented VISUALERASE, which formulates robust concept erasure as visual trajectory erasure.
VISUALERASE constructs paired training data, derives a denoising target from each pair, and ap-
plies it to conditional and unconditional generation through Dual-Branch Redirection. Counterpart
Retention preserves general generation capability. Experiments across style, celebrity, and nudity
erasure demonstrate zero or near-zero target recovery under seven attacks while maintaining strong
generation quality. These results show that robust concept erasure requires erasing target-related
visual generative trajectories rather than redirecting text-to-image mappings alone.

A
PAIRED DATA CONSTRUCTION DETAILS
A.1
CONSTRUCTION PROTOCOL
The paired data used by VISUALERASE are designed to provide instance-aligned visual supervision.
Given a concept-bearing source image xsrc, the counterpart xref should remove the target concept
while preserving the source-specific scene, composition, geometry, and other non-target content.
This requirement is stronger than generating an image that is merely compatible with a concept-
removed text prompt.
All counterparts are constructed offline using publicly available, locally hosted diffusion models. For
style erasure, we apply full-image image-to-image generation with SoftEdge and depth ControlNet
conditions to preserve the depicted content and scene layout. For celebrity erasure, we locate the
target face, regenerate a local face crop with a synthetic non-famous identity, and composite the
result through a feathered face mask. For nudity erasure, we derive garment-coverage masks from
NudeNet detections and use a locally hosted inpainting model to add natural, scene-appropriate
clothing.
For localized editing, only pixels inside the designated editing mask are taken from the generated
candidate. Pixels outside the mask are restored directly from the source image. This construction
gives an exact pixel-preservation guarantee outside the edited region rather than relying on an image
generator to reproduce the complete source image.
A.2
REPRODUCIBLE TAYLOR SWIFT CONSTRUCTION
We use the Taylor Swift subset as a concrete example of the celebrity-pair construction process.
For each source image, GCD is used to identify the target face. We extract a square crop around
the detected face using a crop scale of 1.80 and resize it to 512 × 512. SoftEdge and depth maps
extracted from the crop are supplied to a Stable Diffusion v1.5 image-to-image model.
The positive prompt follows the common template:
RAW candid photograph of a real young adult woman aged
25, a different non-famous identity, [facial-structure
descriptor], natural skin pores and subtle imperfections,
coherent eyes nose and mouth, realistic proportions, natural
makeup, same head pose gaze expression hairstyle lighting
and camera perspective.
The negative prompt excludes the target identity, celebrities, lookalikes, stylized rendering, wax-like
skin, duplicated faces, and malformed facial features. The facial-structure descriptor is not sampled
randomly. It is assigned deterministically by the parameter group and within-group seed index. For
c72, j = 0 uses “a soft oval facial structure,” and j = 1 uses “a naturally round facial structure.”
For c82, j = 0 uses “a gentle square jaw,” and j = 1 uses “a heart-shaped facial structure.” For
c88, j = 0 uses “subtle high cheekbones,” and j = 1 uses “a broad natural facial structure.” Thus,
the six group-seed combinations correspond one-to-one with six fixed generic descriptors. None
specifies another real person.
We use three fixed parameter groups:
c72 = (s = 0.72, λedge = 0.20, λdepth = 0.25, ectrl = 0.50),
c82 = (s = 0.82, λedge = 0.20, λdepth = 0.20, ectrl = 0.50),

A.3
PRACTICAL CONSTRAINTS OF PROPRIETARY IMAGE GENERATORS
We additionally investigated whether a proprietary GPT-based image-generation service could
replace the local construction pipeline.
Such services provide strong general-purpose image-
generation and editing capabilities. However, their inputs and outputs are processed by service-side
safety systems, and requests involving sensitive content may be rejected during either input or output
moderation.4
We first submitted a representative Taylor Swift source image together with a privacy-preserving de-
identification instruction. Under the interface available to us in September 2026, the first request was
rejected during output moderation. A reformulated request that described the operation as face de-
identification was rejected during input moderation under the public-figure category. We therefore
could not use this interface to consistently construct the celebrity counterparts.
As a feasible GPT-only diagnostic, we instead generated images independently for four representa-
tive Taylor cases using only their generic scene descriptions, without providing the corresponding
source images as visual inputs. Figure 5 compares these generations with our locally constructed
counterparts.
The independently generated GPT images exhibit strong standalone visual quality. Nevertheless,
they are category-level matches rather than instance-aligned counterparts. Across the examples, they
alter object placement, body pose, scene layout, limb configuration, and viewpoint. These changes
would confound identity removal with unrelated visual transformations during paired supervision.
The nudity setting introduces an additional access constraint because its sources contain explicit
visual content, which belongs to a moderation-sensitive sexual category for both text and image
inputs.5 We did not upload these images to the proprietary service or attempt to circumvent its
safeguards. Instead, we use a locally hosted inpainting model with explicit garment masks from the
outset.
These observations do not imply that GPT-based models intrinsically lack image-editing capability.
Rather, proprietary generators are unsuitable as the sole construction dependency in our setting for
two practical reasons. Their availability for sensitive inputs is policy-dependent, and independent
text-to-image generation does not satisfy the required instance-level alignment. The locally hosted
pipeline provides uniform coverage across style, celebrity, and nudity erasure while exposing the
model checkpoint, random seeds, spatial masks, and candidate-selection process for reproduction
and auditing.

SALIENCY-LOCALIZED UPDATING
Nudity exhibits greater variation in pose, anatomy, composition, and rendering than style or celebrity
identity. Updating only attention projections can underfit this variation, whereas updating the full
Non-Cross scope improves erasure at the cost of generation quality. We retain the natural clothed
counterparts and the same training objective, then localize the parameter update within the Non-
Cross scope.
We construct a fixed element-wise saliency mask inspired by SalUn (Fan et al., 2024). Let ΘNC
denote the candidate set of U-Net linear and convolutional parameters outside the cross-attention
layers. For each parameter θi ∈ΘNC, we estimate its importance on the pretrained model using the
magnitude of the Dual-Branch Redirection gradient:
