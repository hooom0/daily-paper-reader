# Probability flow ODEs in score-based and reflected diffusion models

Mathematical Institute, University of Oxford 

# Abstract 

Probability-flow ordinary differential equations (PF-ODEs) are widely used as deterministic samplers for score-based diffusion models. Their usual justification is that the Fokker–Planck equation of a diffusion can be rewritten as a continuity equation driven by the score function of the forward diffusion. This identity does not, however, guarantee that the resulting velocity field generates a well-posed flow. We provide theoretical insights into the design of such deterministic samplers for generative models based on diffusions and reflected diffusions. We identify sufficient conditions for a regular Lagrangian PF-ODE flow to exist; reverse sampling and invertibility require two-sided divergence control. For learned scores, sampler stability is controlled by an unweighted velocity error, exposing a mismatch with density-weighted score matching and motivating architectural control of Jacobians, divergence, growth, and compression. Under the manifold hypothesis, positive-time regularization justifies an early-stopped PF-ODE while constants deteriorate near the data endpoint; an explicit sphere example shows that the exact deterministic flow becomes singular as the noise level vanishes even though the diffusion marginals remain well defined. These theoretical insights translate into concrete design principles for stable, in-vertible, and constraint-preserving diffusion samplers. We illustrate the practical relevance of these design principles using controlled numerical experiments. 

# 1 Probability-flow ODEs in diffusion models 

Score-based diffusion models [19] generate data by reversing a noising process and may be sampled either with a reverse-time SDE [3, 5] or with the deterministic probability-flow ODE (PF-ODE) introduced in Song et al. [18]. The PF-ODE is attractive because it removes stochastic simulation, supports deterministic encoding/decoding, and permits likelihood calculations [18, 19]. In constrained generative modeling, reflected diffusion models [12, 15] keep the forward process inside a prescribed domain and can likewise be paired with a deterministic PF-ODE. Contributions. We use theoretical insights from [7, 8] to derive implications for the design and training of score-based and reflected diffusion models, and exhibit explicit examples and sampler diagnostics. First, when data is supported on a lower dimensional manifold, positive-time diffusion regularizes the singular data law but the PF-ODE may become singular as t ↓ 0. In Section 3 we give an explicit example of this type, which leads to collapse of the normal directions at the data endpoint. Second, for learned scores, deterministic sampler stability depends not only on score-matching error but also on regularity, divergence, growth, and compression of the induced velocity field. Third, for reflected models, the PF-ODE contains no explicit reflection term: confinement follows from tangency of the probability-flow velocity, rather than from a zero-normal-score condition. These observations lead to a common design principle: the PF-ODE should be treated as a learned dynamical system with its own well-posedness and conditioning requirements, rather than as an automatic consequence of score estimation. In particular, early stopping, velocity-field regularization, divergence control, low-density validation, and appropriate boundary constraints should be viewed as structural components of deterministic diffusion sampling. We illustrate these principles with numerical experiments (Section 6 and Appendix B.1), with a focus on endpoint conditioning, the DSM–velocity-error gap, and Jacobian regularization.

# 3 Singularity at the data endpoint under the manifold hypothesis 

By data endpoint we mean the zero-noise time t = 0 , not the boundary of a spatial domain. The manifold hypothesis posits that high-dimensional data concentrate on, or near, a set whose intrinsic dimension is much smaller than the ambient dimension. We focus here on the idealized but important limiting case in which the data distribution is exactly supported on a lower-dimensional manifold and is therefore singular with respect to ambient Lebesgue measure. The conclusions below need not hold in the same form for distributions that merely concentrate near a manifold while retaining a positive ambient density. Diffusion models can nevertheless be well behaved in this singular setting. Convergence to targets supported on lower-dimensional manifolds or empirical measures has been established without assuming an ambient target density [10]; more recent results show that score-learning and sampling rates can depend primarily on intrinsic rather than ambient dimension [4, 17]. The question addressed here is different: does such favorable distributional behavior imply that the deterministic probability-flow ODE remains a regular and invertible ambient-space flow all the way to t = 0 ? The following example shows that it does not. The construction is a higher-dimensional version of the time-zero vacuum mechanism in [7]: diffusion immediately fills directions in which the initial law has no ambient density, while the corresponding deterministic probability-flow velocity becomes singular as this regularization is removed.

Relation to convergence results under the manifold hypothesis. De Bortoli [10] showed that denoising diffusion models can converge to targets supported on lower-dimensional manifolds or empirical measures and obtained quantitative W1 guarantees without assuming an ambient target density. Azangulov et al. [4] obtain score-learning and reverse-SDE sampling rates largely inde-pendent of ambient dimension, while Potaptchik et al. [17] showed that the number of reverse-SDE discretization steps required for KL convergence can scale linearly, up to logarithmic factors, with intrinsic dimension. These are distributional convergence results for stochastic diffusion samplers. Proposition 1 addresses a different question: whether the associated deterministic probability flow remains a regular and invertible ambient-space flow as the noise level vanishes. The sphere example shows that, even with the exact score, approaching a lower-dimensional target forces collapse of normal directions. Under the manifold hypothesis, when the target distribution is idealized as being exactly supported on a lower-dimensional manifold, the zero-noise limit should be viewed as a 

singular collapse limit , rather than as an ordinary well-conditioned ODE time. If stable deterministic transport, invertible encoding/decoding, or change-of-variables likelihood evaluation is required, a natural strategy is to stop the PF-ODE at some δ > 0 and treat the passage from pδ to the data distribution separately, for example through a terminal denoising or manifold-valued correction step. If reverse trajectories are integrated closer to zero, one should expect normal Jacobian factors and ambient volume elements to collapse, while the conditioning constants of the deterministic flow deteriorate even for a perfect score. This is an intrinsic geometric consequence of removing diffusion regularization, not merely an effect of score-estimation error.

5.2 Endpoint and boundary failure modes 

Two failure mechanisms clarify what a constrained sampler must control. 

Data-endpoint singularity. Reflected Brownian motion started from a compactly supported density inside an interval has a unique smooth positive density for every t > 0 and satisfies tangency at the walls, yet the score in the initial vacuum still behaves like 1/t [8]. The PF-ODE has no regular Lagrangian flow from time zero, while it is well posed on every [δ, T ] [8]. Thus reflection does not cure the data-endpoint pathology; early stopping remains necessary for compactly supported data. 

Boundary-current compression. For more general no-flux density–flux pairs, if the density degenerates at the boundary then p v · ν = 0 no longer implies v · ν = 0 . A boundary current can coexist with exact marginal transport and confined integral curves while producing unbounded compression and no regular Lagrangian flow [8]. Uniformly elliptic reflected VE/VP models after positive-time regularization avoid this mechanism because their densities are positive up to the boundary, but the example is a warning for learned constrained flows: preserving mass and keeping sample paths inside the domain are not sufficient for the stability of the deterministic sampler. Geometry also enters through score regularity. Smooth convex domains are favorable for Neumann/co-normal regularity, whereas nonconvex or nonsmooth boundaries can degrade second-derivative estimates near the wall [8]. Domain choice and boundary representation are therefore part of the conditioning of a reflected score model, not merely semantic constraints on the output space.

These results suggest treating the PF-ODE as a learned dynamical system, not a by-product of score estimation. The central object is the learned probability-flow velocity, not the score in isolation.

For reflected models the same principle interacts with geometry. If the constraint set is a modeling choice, a smooth geometric representation can therefore improve score regularity and deterministic sampling. These design principles, summarized in Table 1, suggest that validation protocols for ODE-based diffusion models should, in addition to score loss and sample-quality metrics, evaluate: (i) the time profile of Jacobian/divergence norms of vθ ; (ii) empirical volume compression along trajectories; (iii) sensitivity of the sampler to decreasing δ; (iv) score/velocity errors stratified by density level; and, for constrained models, (v) the boundary residual |vθ · ν|. These diagnostics target quantities that affect the flow and can reveal failures invisible to density-weighted training loss. The results also separate two modeling choices that are often conflated. The reverse SDE is a stochastic process whose score error is naturally averaged under its evolving law, while the PF-ODE is a deterministic vector field whose global trajectories can be affected by low-density regions. A score architecture and schedule optimized for SDE sampling need not be optimal for ODE sampling. If deterministic decoding, fast ODE generation, or exact likelihoods are central goals, PF-ODE conditioning should be included explicitly in training and schedule selection. 8References

# B Practical checklist for a PF-ODE implementation 

For an implementation intended to use deterministic PF-ODE sampling, the theory motivates the following checks in addition to ordinary score-model evaluation: 1. report the data-endpoint truncation δ and the terminal denoising procedure; 2. measure Jacobian/divergence norms of vθ over time and across density quantiles; 10 3. test sensitivity of sample trajectories and likelihood estimates to the ODE solver and to perturba-tions of sθ ;4. if invertibility is used, test both forward and reverse compression rather than forward generation alone; 5. for reflected models, evaluate vθ · ν on boundary samples and use the drift-dependent co-normal target; 6. stratify score error by density level so that tail or boundary errors are not hidden by density weighting.
