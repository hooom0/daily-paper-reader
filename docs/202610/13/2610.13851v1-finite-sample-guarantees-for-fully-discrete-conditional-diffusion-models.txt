# Finite-Sample Guarantees for Fully Discrete Conditional Diffusion Models 

Qiuyun Zhu 

Department of Statistics and Actuarial Science University of Iowa 

qiuyun-zhu@uiowa.edu 

Abstract 

We establish finite-sample guarantees for conditional diffusion models learned from data and implemented with finitely many denoising steps. Our approach aligns the score-training objective with the sampling grid, allowing statistical estimation and numerical discretization errors to be analyzed together. Under a smooth conditional density model, we derive high-probability bounds on the generated distribution’s error and quantify the required number of score evaluations. For every smoothness index β > 0, a suitable grid yields averaged conditional Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence ˜Op(n−2β/ (dx+dy +2 β)) relative to the original target with or without early stopping. Both the full and early-stopped samplers attain average target total-variation (TV) error ˜Op(n−β/ (dx+dy +2 β)). Here n is the sample size, dx, d y are the data and conditioning dimensions, and errors are averaged over the conditioning variable. The total-variation bound matches the rate established in existing conditional distribution-estimation theory, up to logarithmic factors. 

# 1 Introduction 

Score-based diffusion models learn to reverse a process that gradually adds noise to data (Ho et al., 2020; Song et al., 2021). A neural network estimates the score, the gradient of the noisy log-density, and a numerical sampler uses this estimate to transform Gaussian noise into an observation (Vincent, 2011). A guarantee for the generated distribution must therefore account for both learning and finite-step sampling. In conditional generation, each observation is paired with side information, such as a class label or covariates. The diffusion perturbs the observation while keeping its condition fixed. In this work, we study how the training sample size, conditional network complexity, and number of score evaluations jointly determine the accuracy of this procedure. 

1.1 Prior Art 

Score Learning and Statistical Guarantees. Score matching avoids the density normalizing constant (Hyvärinen, 2005), while denoising score matching provides an observable perturbation score for training (Vincent, 2011). For unconditional diffusion, Oko et al. (2023) establish nearly minimax distribution-estimation rates over smoothness classes, and Zhang et al. (2024) remove density-lower-bound assumptions. Complementary analyses address neural training-sample complexity (Gupta et al., 2024), gradient-based optimization and generalization (Han et al., 2024), and optimal score estimation by empirical Bayes smoothing under sub-Gaussian tails and score regularity (Wibisono et al., 2024). These works clarify how data determine score accuracy. For a fully discrete conditional 1arXiv:2610.13851v1 [stat.ML] 9 Oct 2026 generator, that accuracy must be controlled at the particular times used by the numerical sampler. As such, we analyze a conditional score network fitted by minimizing a step-weighted denoising loss on the sampling grid, directly connecting its finite-sample error to the updates that generate an observation. 

Conditional Distribution Estimation. Li et al. (2022) provide a classical statistical benchmark for conditional density estimation, deriving minimax rates for a kernel-based estimator under smoothness in the generated variable and total-variation smoothness in the conditioning variable. Their results motivate accounting for regularity in both variables when analyzing conditional diffusion. In the diffusion setting, Fu et al. (2024) obtain smoothness-dependent TV rates under a positive Gaussian-factor density assumption, while Tang et al. (2025) allow different smoothness levels in the two variables and establish conditional TV rates under compact-support and density-lower-bound assumptions, together with manifold-adaptive Wasserstein guarantees. Tian and Shen (2026) establish conditional TV and square-root-KL guarantees under a positive Gaussian-factor model, including time-zero generation for smoothness greater than one. These statistical guarantees concern continuous-time conditional diffusion: in Tian and Shen (2026), the reverse process is continuous before a final interval with a fixed score evaluation. In contrast, we provide a finite-sample guarantee for a fully discrete conditional generator, with learning and sampling analyzed on the same finite grid. This distinction is essential because an error bound integrated over diffusion time does not, by itself, control the score at the sampler’s selected evaluation times. We retain the corresponding smoothness-dependent statistical exponent while explicitly accounting for every numerical update and the number of score evaluations. Moreover, our early-stopped guarantee controls the KL divergence from the original conditional data distribution, rather than only its smoothed version, for every β > 0 (including the low-smoothness range 0 < β ≤ 1). 

Sampling with Estimated Scores. Given an accurate score estimate, Lee et al. (2023); Chen et al. (2023b) establish convergence guarantees for diffusion samplers. Chen et al. (2023a) bound the KL divergence between the data distribution (or its Gaussian-smoothed version) and the generated distribution by three sources of error: initializing from a Gaussian rather than the exact terminal distribution, using an estimated score, and approximating the reverse process with finite time steps. Refined dimension and step bounds are obtained by Benton et al. (2024); Li and Yan (2025). Chen et al. (2026) develop alternative algorithms with polylogarithmic dependence on inverse accuracy, given sufficiently accurate scores. These results quantify sampling error in terms of prescribed score accuracy, rather than the training sample size. Huang et al. (2026) connect learning and discrete sampling for unconditional residual score networks and Euler–Maruyama updates, including an explicit optimization gap. For conditional generation, an essential question is how to translate improved learning accuracy into improved output accuracy without letting numerical error dominate. We address this question by choosing the conditional network class and exponential-integrator grid together, so that finite-step sampling preserves the smoothness-dependent statistical rate. For a given number of training pairs, our bound specifies how many denoising steps suffice to attain this accuracy, with one score-network evaluation per step. The guarantee applies to both early-stopped and full sampling. 

1.2 Motivation and Challenges 

Our motivation is to determine whether fully discrete conditional generation can retain the statistical accuracy of continuous-time models. From Section 1.1, we see that prior work establishes smoothness-dependent statistical rates for continuous-time conditional diffusion (Fu et al., 2024; Tang et al., 22025; Tian and Shen, 2026), while numerical sampling analyses bound the output error given a prescribed score accuracy (Chen et al., 2023a). The continuous-time guarantees do not account for discretization of the full reverse trajectory, and prescribed-score bounds do not establish the training sample size needed to achieve that accuracy. This motivates a conditional generation theory that connects finite training data to output accuracy and quantifies how many denoising steps suffice to preserve the statistical rate. Achieving this goal requires overcoming three challenges. Connecting learning to generation requires score-error control at the sampler’s actual evaluation times, and an integrated-time bound alone does not ensure this control. The sampling grid must also keep numerical error within the statistical error bound. Finally, we must compare both early-stopped and full outputs with the original conditional distribution, rather than only a smoothed law, even in the low-smoothness range 0 < β ≤ 1. These challenges lead to our central research question: 

Can a fully discrete conditional diffusion model learned from training data attain smoothness-dependent accuracy relative to the original conditional data distribution with an explicit denoising-step count, while satisfying the same KL error bound with and without early stopping? 

1.3 Main Contribution 

We establish that fully discrete conditional diffusion can attain smoothness-dependent statistical accuracy, with an explicit sampling budget determined by the training sample size. For every smoothness index β > 0, both early-stopped and full sampling satisfy the same averaged conditional KL error bound relative to the original conditional data distribution. Specifically, our contribution is detailed as follows. 1. Learning guarantees for finite-step generation. We replace prescribed score accuracy with a finite-sample guarantee for the conditional score actually learned on the sampling grid (cf. Theorem 4). This resolves the mismatch between integrated-time learning guarantees and finite-step generation, bringing the score evaluations used by the sampler under direct statistical control. 2. Guarantees for the original conditional data distribution. We establish KL error bounds against the original conditional data distribution for both early-stopped and full sampling, covering every β > 0 (cf. Proposition 7). In particular, our early-stopping guarantee controls the unsmoothed conditional data distribution even in the low-smoothness regime 0 < β ≤ 1, rather than merely a Gaussian-smoothed approximation. 3. Statistical accuracy preserved under full discretization. We prove that numerical sampling need not degrade the smoothness-dependent statistical accuracy of the learned model. Both stopping rules achieve the same KL error bound and TV convergence rate, and we specify the denoising-step budget sufficient to attain these guarantees as the training sample grows (cf. Theorem 8).

# 5 Discussion 

We established finite-sample guarantees for fully discrete conditional diffusion by analyzing score learning and numerical sampling within a single framework. The resulting bounds relate the amount of training data to both generation accuracy and an explicit denoising step count. Under the stated density assumptions, the full and early-stopped samplers achieve the same averaged conditional KL error bound relative to the original conditional data distribution for every smoothness index 

β > 0. Thus, the analysis shows how to preserve statistical accuracy from score estimation through finite-step generation. 10 This framework opens several directions for further development. Incorporating optimization and artificial-noise Monte Carlo errors would extend the analysis to practical training procedures. Data-adaptive time grids and higher-order integrators could further reduce sampling effort at a prescribed statistical accuracy. Exploiting low-dimensional or factorizable conditional structure is another promising direction for sharpening the dependence on dimension.
