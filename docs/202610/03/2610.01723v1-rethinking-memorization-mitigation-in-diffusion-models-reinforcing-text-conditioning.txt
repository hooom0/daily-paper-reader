ABSTRACT
Text-to-image diffusion models have achieved remarkable progress in image syn-
thesis, yet can exhibit memorization by closely reproducing individual training ex-
amples. Effective mitigation must preserve useful prompt information to guide al-
ternative depictions. We introduce a training-free method that redistributes cross-
attention with Gaussian smoothing before reinforcing content-token contributions
and attenuating padding contributions, without additional denoiser evaluations.
With this intervention, stronger content conditioning can improve prompt align-
ment at comparable training-image similarity. A local analysis identifies when
reinforcement preserves shared value information while redistribution reduces lo-
calized attention mass. On Stable Diffusion v1.4 and v2.0, all evaluated smooth-
ing widths lie on the empirical Pareto frontiers for training-image similarity versus
both prompt alignment and image preference. A configuration selected on Stable
Diffusion reduces template reproduction in DeepFloyd IF without further tuning.
These findings support jointly controlling conditioning allocation and strength to
generate prompt-consistent alternatives.

INTRODUCTION
Text-to-image diffusion models enable high-quality image synthesis from natural-language descrip-
tions (Ho et al., 2020; Song et al., 2021b; Rombach et al., 2022; Ramesh et al., 2022; Saharia et al.,
2022). Yet these models can exhibit memorization: certain prompts elicit close reproductions of
individual training images (Carlini et al., 2023; Somepalli et al., 2023a;b; Webster, 2023). Such
reproduction can preserve a training image’s distinctive composition and visual details, beyond the
semantic resemblance expected from depicting the same subject. Mitigating this behavior requires
more than moving the generated image away from a training reference: the model must still produce
a useful alternative that follows the prompt. A prompt generally permits many valid depictions, and
its objects, attributes, and context provide information for constructing them. Figure 1 illustrates
this goal: the film context in the Emma Watson/Belle prompt supports an alternative depiction of
the subject.
Several inference-time approaches mitigate memorization by weakening or avoiding conditional
responses associated with reproduction. Prompt-embedding adjustment reduces the magnitude of
text-conditional guidance (Wen et al., 2024), while initial-noise adjustment seeks starting points with
weaker conditional responses and earlier escape from memorization basins (Han et al., 2025). Other
approaches postpone or reverse guidance (Jain et al., 2025), or reduce attention to prompt words
(Ren et al., 2024). We instead make content reinforcement an active part of mitigation: strengthening
useful prompt information to guide alternative depictions.
We find that content reinforcement can improve prompt alignment at comparable training-image
similarity. This improvement occurs when content gain is increased with redistribution and padding
attenuation held fixed, showing that reinforcement can support useful conditioning during mitiga-
tion. We implement this approach as a training-free cross-attention intervention that combines con-
tent amplification with Gaussian redistribution and selective padding attenuation. Early smoothing
changes the allocation of attention across token positions; amplification then strengthens the redis-
tributed content contributions. The intervention retains the original prompt and frozen model and
requires no additional denoiser evaluations.

RELATED WORK
Memorization in diffusion models.
Training-image extraction and replication studies show that
diffusion models can reproduce individual training examples (Carlini et al., 2023; Somepalli et al.,
2023a). Data duplication and image–caption associations influence this behavior (Somepalli et al.,
2023b;a; Chen et al., 2024). Mechanistic analyses locate memorization-related neurons in cross-
attention layers (Hintersdorf et al., 2024), while geometric analyses explain memorization through
the relationship between learned and data manifolds (Ross et al., 2025). Reproduction also takes
different forms: Webster (2023) documents both image and template reproduction, and Bright End-
ing (Chen et al., 2025) examines local memorization in which only part of an image is copied.
These perspectives provide complementary accounts of what is reproduced and how memorization
is represented in the model.
Inference-time mitigation.
Existing methods intervene at different points in generation. Ran-
dom prompt perturbations disrupt the conditioning associated with reproduction (Somepalli et al.,
2023b), while gradient-based prompt adjustment optimizes text embeddings to reduce the magni-
tude of text-conditional noise predictions (Wen et al., 2024). Guidance-based methods delay or re-
verse classifier-free guidance to escape memorization basins (Jain et al., 2025); Anti-Memorization
Guidance combines guidance terms targeting image duplication, caption duplication, and prompt
specificity (Chen et al., 2024). Initial-noise adjustment searches for starting points that permit ear-
lier escape from memorization basins (Han et al., 2025). Bright Ending (Chen et al., 2025) localizes
copied regions using EOS-token attention at the final denoising step and uses the resulting spatial
mask to focus detection and prompt optimization on those regions. For direct attention modification,
Ren et al. (2024) combine summary-token masking with an increase in the start-of-sequence (SOS)
token’s logit before softmax; the latter shifts attention toward SOS and reduces the attention weights
assigned to prompt words. Our method instead redistributes attention across token positions be-

fore reinforcing content contributions, preserving the original prompt and initial noise without input
optimization.
Attention control for semantic fidelity.
Cross-attention control also supports the expression of
prompt semantics. Prompt-to-Prompt (Hertz et al., 2023) reuses attention maps to preserve spatial
layout during text-based image editing. Structured Diffusion (Feng et al., 2023) incorporates lin-
guistic structure into cross-attention guidance to improve compositional generation. SynGen (Rassin
et al., 2023) uses syntactic relations to align the attention maps of entities and their modifiers, im-
proving attribute binding. Attend-and-Excite (Chefer et al., 2023) addresses neglected subjects by
smoothing their spatial attention maps and iteratively optimizing the latent to strengthen their acti-
vations. We share the goal of making generated images more faithfully reflect prompt content. Our
intervention redistributes attention along token positions and directly amplifies the resulting content
contributions, coupling this semantic objective with memorization mitigation rather than optimizing
a subject-specific spatial attention objective.

REINFORCING REDISTRIBUTED CROSS-ATTENTION
We reinforce prompt content to support useful alternatives during mitigation, while controlling its
allocation across token positions (Figure 1). We first redistribute attention, then reinforce content
within the resulting pattern and selectively attenuate padding contributions. This order makes am-
plification act on a new mixture of token values. We formalize the operator, analyze reinforcement
with localized dilution, and adapt it to DeepFloyd IF.

changes. To interpret the retained mass, consider a fixed additive value vector present at every

is proportional to the mass remaining on those positions. The two bounds below thus describe how a
shared feature can be preserved while a localized contribution is attenuated. The analysis separates
variation in the shared feature from the fraction of mass returned to the original set.

controls a concrete balance between dilution and retention of shared value information. The propo-

its consequences for image generation are assessed empirically. Section 4.3 tests content reinforce-
ment within our method, and Sections 4.4 and 4.5 examine the roles of allocation and guidance
magnitude.
3.3
TRANSFER TO DEEPFLOYD IF
IF uses T5 conditioning and attention over added text keys and image keys. We apply the same
operator to Stage I’s text-key block. Because T5 has no CLIP start token, smoothing spans the entire
text block; content positions precede EOS, and positions after EOS receive λp. Image keys are
unchanged, and Stage II uses its original attention. The transfer uses the SD-selected configuration
without IF-specific strength tuning. This adaptation tests the design with a different text encoder
and pixel-space denoising while retaining the same redistribution-and-reinforcement rule.

EXPERIMENTS
We evaluate whether redistribution and reinforcement can jointly reduce training-image similarity
while retaining prompt-consistent generation. After benchmarking the suppression–quality trade-
off, we test whether stronger content conditioning remains useful during mitigation (Figure 4),
whether redistribution improves the trade-off at fixed token gains (Table 1), and whether the ef-
fect is explained by guidance magnitude alone (Table 2). We then examine the EOS suppression
boundary, transfer to a different model family, and selective application with an existing detector.

4.7
TRANSFER TO DEEPFLOYD IF
We test whether our SD-selected configuration transfers to T5-conditioned, pixel-space IF with-
out target-specific tuning. Using template annotations (Webster, 2023), we evaluate 48 template-
verbatim (TV) prompts across 11 shared-reference groups and three seeds (144 images per arm). As
shown in Table 3, our method reduces template matches from 38.89% to 20.14%. This reduction
under the SD-selected settings shows that the intervention can suppress reproduction with a different
text encoder and generation space. CLIP and ImageReward also decrease relative to the base model,
so the transferred configuration retains a suppression benefit with a quality cost.
4.8
SELECTIVE MITIGATION WITH AN EXISTING DETECTOR
To limit intervention on ordinary prompts, we intervene at full strength (σ = 4) only on inputs
flagged by the initial-step guidance detector of Wen et al. (2024). At a calibrated 5% false-positive
rate, mean SSCD on 500 memorized prompts falls from 0.5486 to 0.3475, versus 0.2078 when every
prompt is treated. Average ImageReward changes by −0.0065 over normal prompts and −0.1291
within the flagged normal subset. Because unflagged inputs follow the original generation path,
selective application preserves most normal generations while still reducing reproduction. Its weaker
suppression than universal application reflects the memorization-prone prompts left untreated.
5
CONCLUSION
We show that stronger content conditioning within redistributed attention can improve prompt align-
ment at comparable training-image similarity, making reinforcement an active means of generating
useful alternatives during mitigation. The local analysis identifies conditions under which rein-
forcement preserves shared value information while redistribution dilutes localized attention, and
controlled comparisons show quality recovery from redistribution at fixed token gains. Our method
attains empirical Pareto-frontier performance on two Stable Diffusion benchmarks. An SD-selected
configuration also reduces template reproduction in DeepFloyd IF without further tuning. The result-
ing principle is to reinforce useful prompt content during mitigation, using redistribution to improve
the balance between reproduction suppression and prompt-consistent generation.

Relation to the experimental design.
The gain interval couples dilution to coherence. Wider
smoothing can reduce retention on a localized set while increasing the transport-distance bound on
feature distortion. Content reinforcement compensates for the latter within the admissible interval.
After the early smoothing window, G = I and the local interval disappears; subsequent reweight-
ing acts on attention along the already modified trajectory. The analysis therefore motivates early
redistribution coupled with reinforcement, while the reported schedule and gains are empirical de-
sign choices. The gain sweep and the row-mass and direction/norm controls assess the resulting
allocation–strength trade-off through prompt alignment, image preference, and training-image sim-
ilarity.
Computational checks.
Numerical verification covers the finite-kernel inequalities, complete-row
gain conditions, and transport bound, including equality-achieving profiles. Independent linear-
programming checks confirm the sharp bound in Eq. (17). Verification scripts and detailed numerical
results are included with the manuscript source.
Four counterexamples delimit the analysis: useful information confined to the same isolated token
can be attenuated; a uniform attention pattern can be amplified everywhere; reflection can change
total row mass; and background inflow can outweigh a localized component’s dilution. These cases
motivate the coherence, concentration, and full-row conditions used above.

MECHANISM CONTROLS AND INTERPRETATION
The direction–norm controls in Section 4.5 use the row-normalized content-positional setting
(abl position norm1), with σc = 1, ρ = 1, α = 1, λw = 2, and λp = 0.1. Mixing is re-
stricted to the content span with donor-normalized Gaussian positional weights, and the reweighted
attention rows are normalized. This fixes attention mass, while the same-state swaps in Eq. (9) sep-
arate guidance direction and magnitude. Table 2 reports the direction–norm decomposition of this
setting. The main Gaussian operator uses reflected smoothing over all non-start positions and retains
the reweighted row mass. The controls require two denoiser evaluations per step to obtain base and
intervened guidance at the same latent. Sampling with our method retains the original number of
denoiser evaluations.
Equation (9) is evaluated separately at each current latent. In mixed precision, the realized direc-
tion/norm controls have maximum relative norm errors of approximately 2.02% and 1.54%, respec-
tively; the corresponding means are 0.019% and 0.029%.
The mechanism analysis measures the conditional-guidance vector ϵc −ϵu and its effect on
generated-image similarity. The entropy diagnostic below measures the discrete distribution over
token weights.
