ABSTRACT
Although diffusion models produce high-quality images, they also reproduce and
amplify demographic imbalances in their training data. Debiasing their generation
process post-training w.r.t. some sensitive attribute usually relies on classifier guid-
ance or explicit text extra-conditioning, but this reduces methods’ applicability and
output diversity. Conversely, methods promoting diversity alone do not ensure fair
attribute representation. In this paper, we propose a method tackling fairness and
diversity jointly that is generally applicable to any diffusion model and any sensitive
attribute. To this end, an adapter connects the frozen diffusion model to a pre-
trained vision-language embedding space, enabling fairness and diversity guidance
without sensitive-attribute annotations. For fairness, pairs of text prompts define
attribute directions which guide batch composition towards specific proportions.
For diversity, we introduce a score measuring disagreement between the semantic
estimates derived from this representation. The formulation supports uncondi-
tional and text-conditional diffusion models, while requiring no prior knowledge
or data of sensitive attribute. Experiments confirm that our method improves qual-
ity and diversity scores at comparable fairness levels. Our code is available at
https://github.com/theaudaudiffret/Debias_anything.

INTRODUCTION
Diffusion (Ho et al., 2020; Song et al., 2021b), and equivalently flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023),
models are the standard unconditional generation models or text-to-image systems deployed at scale
(Rombach et al., 2022; Podell et al., 2024; Wu et al., 2025). They inherit imbalances of their training
data and often exaggerate them (Perera & Patel, 2023; Bianchi et al., 2023; Luccioni et al., 2023), and
the guidance they sample with amplifies the imbalance further as its scale grows (Kim et al., 2026).
Debiasing them includes choosing how often a given property, the sensitive attribute, should appear
in the generated images, and making the model comply (Choi et al., 2020; Teo et al., 2023). Two
problems make debiasing diffusion models a hard problem. Firstly, existing methods have limited
applicability with various restrictions: either they must rely on sensitive attribute supervision (Parihar
et al., 2024; Shi et al., 2025; Zaleska et al., 2026), require model finetuning (Shen et al., 2024), are
not adaptable to arbitrary attributes (Vardhana et al., 2026), or are restricted to text-to-image models
(Friedrich et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2023). Secondly, guidance, used to steer a diffusion model
towards fair representation as predicted by an attribute classifier, harms output diversity: the stronger
the push, the more the samples cluster around a few typical images and the rarer ones vanish (Ho &
Salimans, 2022; Sadat et al., 2024; Sehwag et al., 2022). Methods that restore diversity exist (Um &
Ye, 2024; Corso et al., 2024), but they do not aim for specific attribute fairness.
We propose Debias Anything, a guidance that sets the proportion of any multi-class attribute one can
describe in words, with no labels and no generator retraining, and keeps the diversity of the model.
The attribute is described independently of the diffusion model or prompt as short sentences, for
instance a person with eyeglasses and a person without eyeglasses. An image-text encoder places
sentences and images in one shared space, so sentences pairs define a fairness direction which can
be used to steer generated images towards fairness. To follow that direction while images are still
noisy, we train a small adapter, once per model and for no attribute in particular, that predicts the
encoder’s expected embedding of the final image from the inner activations of the frozen generator.
Each sampling step then moves every image along a direction towards an assigned attribute class. A
second term, computed from the same adapter, pushes the images back towards the rare regions the
first term empties.
We compare Debias Anything with published baselines on unconditional face models and a text-to-
image model, demonstrating its versatility. Despite the strong assumptions made by the baselines,
especially regarding attribute supervision, Debias Anything is better than or comparable to them.
On CelebA-HQ, it reaches the best or near-best fairness score on every considered attribute, with
an improved image quality. On Stable Diffusion 1.5, it substantially improves fairness across all

RELATED WORK
Debiasing diffusion models.
Table 1 compares existing methods. Almost all of them share at least
one restriction among four. First, many need attribute supervision like reference images for ITI-GEN
(Zhang et al., 2023) and labeled images for DiffLens (Shi et al., 2025) and ICM (Zaleska et al.,
2026). Second, some cannot debias towards arbitrary attribute proportions (Vardhana et al., 2026).
Third, most rely on text conditioning in the base model, which restricts them to text-to-image models
(Friedrich et al., 2025; Li et al., 2024; Jiang et al., 2025). Fourth, a few of them necessitate retraining
or fine-tuning the base model (Shen et al., 2024). Our method is free from all these assumptions.
The closest methods to ours steer a whole batch towards a target distribution at sampling time.
Balancing Act (Parihar et al., 2024) does so through a predictor trained on labeled U-Net activations,
and every image receives the gradient of a batch-level distance, but remains a supervised method.
Score Guidance (Tiwary et al., 2026) needs exemplar images of each value, except in a text version
that needs no supervision but is less effective in most of the reported experiments. Both versions score
the attribute in pixel space, whereas our score is read in the encoder’s embedding space (Section 4.1),
and both adopt a greedy assignment of images to values early in sampling, whereas ours is optimally
recomputed at every step (Proposition 1).
Restoring diversity.
Guidance narrows the distribution it samples (Ho & Salimans, 2022) and
amplifies attribute imbalance as its scale grows (Kim et al., 2026). Sampling-time methods recover
the tail by pushing towards low-density regions of a feature space (Sehwag et al., 2022), repelling
the samples of a batch in pixel or feature space (Corso et al., 2024; Bhat et al., 2026), maximising
their kernel entropy (Jalali et al., 2025), boosting the initial noise (Um et al., 2025), annealing the
conditioning (Sadat et al., 2024), measuring rarity against a prior independent of the generator (Park
& Um, 2026), or scoring rarity by how much re-denoising deforms the Tweedie estimate, with a
trained classifier (Um et al., 2024) or without one (SGMS; Um & Ye, 2024). None of them constrains
a sensitive attribute. Our rarity term transposes SGMS into the encoder’s space through the same
adapter for efficiency purposes, and it is added to the fairness term so that the tail is recovered at the
target proportion.
Inferring the attribute during sampling.
Classifier guidance (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021) trains a
classifier on noisy images for each attribute; training-free guidance (Chung et al., 2023; Yu et al.,
2023; Bansal et al., 2024; Ye et al., 2024), like the text version of Score Guidance, applies an off-the-
shelf model to the denoised image, a blurred average, early in sampling; readout guidance (Luo et al.,
2024) trains light heads on inner features with task labels. Instead, our method is unsupervised and
only applies an encoder on clean, non-blurry images.
The closest method to ours is that of unsupervised self-debiasing (Vardhana et al., 2026), the only
debiasing method that is given no attribute at all. For each prompt, it trains a projector from the
h-space of Kwon et al. (2023), to an embedding of the image that the sampler produces, then clusters
the projections and steers each batch towards balanced clusters. The attribute is therefore discovered
rather than named, and a target can only be set over the clusters found. Our adapter is instead trained
once per generator rather than per prompt, and is simulation-free: it regresses the embedding of a
clean image from noised versions of that image rather than from the sampling trajectory that produced
it (Section 4.1). Sentences then name the attribute, and our batch assignment can set any proportion.

METHOD
We describe here our guidance method for debiasing a pre-trained diffusion model Dθ of any kind
w.r.t. any sensitive attribute, expressed in free text, with arbitrary proportions and preserved output
quality & diversity, while requiring no extra annotated data and no diffusion model fine-tuning.
For this, we combine two guidance terms, one for fairness and one for diversity, and compute both
from a single semantic representation of the image being generated. The proposed method therefore
contains three pieces (Figure 2): an adapter that makes a clean and easy-to-use embedding space of
a pre-trained image-text encoder available at every noise level of a frozen generator (Section 4.1),
attribute guidance directions from pairs of prompts with the batch-level fairness term (Section 4.2),
and a diversity term defined in that space (Section 4.3).

At the fairness setting weight the discrepancy is the lowest in race and eyeglasses and, in gender,

ABLATIONS ON CELEBA
Results are included in Section D; selected findings are the following. Reading the score through
the adapter is what makes the rare attribute reachable. At equal weights on eyeglasses it drives
the discrepancy from 0.573 to 0.32 while lowering MIND from 20.51 to 8.9, whereas the same
score read from the encoder on the denoised image never passes 0.489 and raises MIND (Table 5
and Figure 6). Every baseline diversity term improves on the fairness term alone, but ours lowers
the discrepancy most (Table 8). On gender all four lower MIND from 2.52 to about 2.0, and our
term lowers the discrepancy from 0.031 to 0.006; on eyeglasses they lower the discrepancy and raise
Vendi, while MIND moves within one standard deviation and FID rises by up to 1.2. Our method
is resilient to changing the wording of attribute reference sentences, as shown in Figure 10.

CONCLUSION
We introduce Debias Anything, a generative model guidance that sets the proportions of a sensitive
attribute described in words, in unconditional and text-to-image diffusion models, with no labels and
no generator retraining. A generic adapter gives access to the embedding space of an image-text
encoder; from it, a batch-level fairness term enforces the target proportions and a diversity term coun-
teracts the loss of diversity that guidance causes. Although it receives very little attribute information,
Debias Anything is better than or comparable to methods that rely on attribute supervision.
Limitations. The method has the limits of its parts. The reconstituted attribute relies on the image-text
embedding, with the encoder’s own biases. Guidance can only push towards images the model can
produce: on Stable Diffusion, male nurses are rare and remain hard to reach. Steering one attribute
moves others, as eyeglasses moved gender on CelebA, so attribute combinations should be considered.
The proportion is enforced on a batch and not at sample level. The two guidance weights have a
narrow useful range that we select by sweeps as detailed in the appendix. Our evidence is on human
faces only, at uniform target proportions, with single seeds on CelebA-HQ and Stable Diffusion.
Future work. We believe that the work has several future directions. The adapter itself could serve
to audit a model’s biases at a fraction of the cost of generating and classifying its samples, or to
stop a biased trajectory early. The same recipe, one adapter from an inner layer to an embedding
shared with text, carries over to video and audio diffusion models, whose encoders exist. Finally, the
proportions we enforce are measured by classifiers; putting the correction of their errors inside the
loop, rather than after it, would let the sampler aim at the true proportion instead of the counted one.

ETHICS STATEMENT
This work makes a generator represent groups of people in proportions an operator chooses, and the
mechanism that sets a proportion to one half can set it to zero. Any method that controls an attribute
offers the same lever, including the label-based methods we compare with. Furthermore, the method
makes a chosen distribution enforceable and says nothing about which distribution is fair.
The attributes we steer (gender, race, age, skin tone and eyeglasses) are named in text and assessed
by classifiers we trained and external tools like FairFace or FID, that carry biases of their own and
may impose fixed categories, binary gender and four races among some of them. Our discrepancy
numbers are only as good as the used classifiers.

Implementation details.
We implemented our method using PyTorch (Paszke et al., 2019) with the
help of SciPy (Virtanen et al., 2020) for the matching algorithm. Our code is available at https://
github.com/theaudaudiffret/Debias_anything. Every run uses one NVIDIA A100
(40 GB) GPU.
B.1
GENERATORS
On CelebA (Liu et al., 2015) we train an EDM model (Karras et al., 2022) at 64 × 64: a U-Net
(Ronneberger et al., 2015) of 19.4 M parameters (base width 96, multipliers (1, 2, 2, 2), two residual
blocks per level in the encoder and three in the decoder, self-attention at 162, 82 and in the middle
block, dropout 0.1), trained with the EDM loss and preconditioning on the 162,770 training images
(centre crop 140, no flip) with Adam (Kingma & Ba, 2015), batch 256, EMA 0.9999 and a learning
rate of 2 · 10−4 divided by 2, 10 and 10 at plateaus of the validation loss; the EMA weights of
lowest validation loss are reached after 1,487 epochs (9.5 · 105 steps, 8.5 days). On CelebA-HQ
(Karras et al., 2018) we use the released P2 model (Choi et al., 2022), an ADM U-Net (Dhariwal &
Nichol, 2021) of 93.6 M parameters at 256 × 256 (width 128, multipliers (1, 1, 2, 2, 4, 4), attention
at 162) with a linear β schedule over 1,000 steps. The text-to-image model is the released Stable
Diffusion 1.5 from Rombach et al. (2022) (860 M-parameter U-Net, 512 × 512).
B.2
ATTRIBUTES, PROMPTS AND SAMPLE SIZES
On CelebA (Liu et al., 2015) the attributes are gender (a photo of a man / a photo of a woman) and
eyeglasses (a person with eyeglasses or sunglasses / a person without eyeglasses or sunglasses). On
CelebA-HQ (Karras et al., 2018), following Balancing Act (Parihar et al., 2024), gender keeps the
CelebA pair, race adds a photo of a black person / a photo of a white person, and eyeglasses is a photo
of a person with eyeglasses / a photo of a person without eyeglasses. All these attributes are binary
and use the form of Section A.2. On Stable Diffusion (Rombach et al., 2022), following DiffLens (Shi
et al., 2025), images are generated from A face of a {doctor, firefighter, nurse, receptionist}. Gender
is binary (a photo of a woman / a photo of a man); age and race use the K-valued form of Section 4.2
with t0 = a photo of a person, and t1, t2, t3 = a photo of a young / middle-aged / old person for age,
t1, . . . , t4 = a photo of a white / black / asian / indian person for race, the four FairFace groups of
the DiffLens protocol. The assignment of Equation (9) is solved with the Hungarian algorithm (Kuhn,
1955) on a cost matrix in which the column of value k is repeated Bk times, so that the quotas are
met exactly. The targets are uniform, αk = 1/K. Each configuration is sampled with five seeds of
5,000 images on CelebA, one seed of 10,000 images per attribute on CelebA-HQ, and one seed of
500 images per prompt on Stable Diffusion.
B.3
SAMPLING
CelebA: deterministic Heun solver (Karras et al., 2022), 100 steps on σ ∈[0.002, 80] (ρ = 7), EMA
weights, guidance for σ ≤σmax = 10 (67 steps), batch size B = 100; SGMS (Um & Ye, 2024) and
our diversity term of Equation (12) act at every fifth network evaluation and re-noise at the fixed level
σs = 0.96 of Equation (11), SigLIPMS (Section A.3) at every evaluation. CelebA-HQ: DDIM (Song
et al., 2021a), 50 steps, η = 1, guidance at every step, batch size B = 50; the unguided model and
Balancing Act (Parihar et al., 2024) are sampled with the released code on the same schedule with
batch size B = 100. Stable Diffusion: Euler with trailing timesteps, 30 steps, classifier-free guidance
(Ho & Salimans, 2022) at scale 7.5, guidance for σ ∈[0.2, 4] (21 steps), batch size B = 100 images
of a single prompt. A run takes 18–25 min per 5,000 images on CelebA, 2.1–2.9 h per 10,000 on
CelebA-HQ and 37 min per 2,000 on Stable Diffusion.

B.5
METRICS
Reference sets.
On CelebA (Liu et al., 2015) the reference has the size of the generated set, drawn
for the diversity experiments from the minority set of SGMS (Um & Ye, 2024), the 10,000 training
images of highest AvgkNN (Sehwag et al., 2022) (5 nearest neighbours, ResNet-50 features (He
et al., 2016)), and for the fairness experiments with replacement from the training set by a sampler
balanced on the attribute. On CelebA-HQ (Karras et al., 2018), as in Balancing Act (Parihar et al.,
2024), it is 5,000 images balanced with the attribute labels for gender and eyeglasses (2,500 per class;
the 1,468 images with eyeglasses are drawn with replacement), and for race, which has no label, the
2,500 images most and the 2,500 least similar to a black person under CLIP ViT-B/32 (Radford et al.,
2021). On Stable Diffusion we follow DiffLens (Shi et al., 2025), which takes FFHQ (Karras et al.,
2019) as the real reference for text-to-image models and computes FID on the 2,000 images of the
four prompts at once.
Quality and diversity.
FID (Heusel et al., 2017) is computed on Inception-V3 features (Szegedy
et al., 2016) on CelebA and CelebA-HQ, and with Clean-FID (Parmar et al., 2022) on Stable Diffusion.
On CelebA we add MIND (Berthet et al., 2026), an optimal-transport distance on the same features
(1,000 random projections, equal set sizes) that drops the Gaussian assumption of FID, known to
misrank diffusion models (Stein et al., 2023) and to bias the estimate with sample size (Chong &
Forsyth, 2020); precision and recall(Kynk¨a¨anniemi et al., 2019), all with five nearest neighbours,
and density and coverage (Naeem et al., 2020), which separate fidelity from coverage; and the Vendi
score (Friedman & Dieng, 2023) with a cosine kernel, which needs no reference. On Stable Diffusion,
CLIP-T and CLIP-I (Radford et al., 2021; Shi et al., 2025) are the similarity of a sample to its prompt
and to the unguided sample of the same prompt; like FD, they are computed per occupation and
averaged over the four.

D.5
DIVERSITY TERMS ALONE
The earliest runs measure each diversity term without the fairness term, against the 10 000 CelebA
training images with the highest average distance to their five nearest neighbours in ResNet-50 feature
space, following Sehwag et al. (2022): against this set a term scores well only if it reaches the rare
part of the data. These runs predate the protocol of the rest of this appendix: σmax = 20, 1000 images
per setting, prompt “a photo of a man”, and every metric below, MIND included, is against this rare
subset and not against the balanced reference of the other sections.
Selecting each term at its lowest MIND would be a mistake. SGMS reaches MIND = 27.6 at
σs = 0.4, wdiv = 200 (Table 14), but its precision has fallen from 0.97 to 0.77 by then and the
images at that setting carry visible artefacts: the term has left the model’s manifold, and the metric,
which only asks how close the samples are to the rare reference images, does not tell that apart
from reaching the tail of the data. Table 10 therefore keeps, for each term, the setting with the
lowest MIND among those whose precision stays at or above 0.95, a floor 0.02 below the unguided
model. Under that floor avgKNN reaches the lowest MIND, 62.7, with the best coverage and Vendi;
SigLIPMS comes second at 71.2 with the best density, PerturbProj third, and SGMS, the term
with the largest recall gain, last at 101.0, because its recall is bought with precision, which the floor
forbids.

QUALITATIVE RESULTS
Every figure of this section shares its seeds across rows, so a column is one starting noise under
several regimes and the panels can be read image by image. Together with Figure 1 they show three
things: the guidance acts on individual images and leaves what it does not name, our method changes
a face more cleanly than a labelled predictor, and the wording of the sentences matters little.
CelebA-HQ against Balancing Act (Figure 9).
Six seeds, three of them women in the unguided
row, under Balancing Act and under Debias Anything at matched discrepancy. Both methods turn the
same two columns, the fifth and sixth, into men, and both leave the first three women and the man in
the fourth column as they are, so the two batches end at the same proportion by the same choice of
images. The difference is in the faces they produce: the man Balancing Act makes in the fifth column
carries a blurred, uneven skin and a distorted mouth, while ours is a clean face with the seed’s hair
and lighting, and the sixth column shows the same gap in a milder form. That is the visible side of
the FID difference in Table 2: at equal discrepancy, guidance through a labelled predictor pays in
image quality where guidance through the adapter does not.
Sensitivity to the sentences (Figure 10).
The same six seeds and the same weight wfair = 20, with
five different pairs of sentences naming gender: woman and man, female and male person, girl’s
and boy’s portrait, lady and gentleman, and close-up photos of a woman and of a man. All five pairs
turn the same two columns, the fifth and sixth, and leave the four others untouched; the five men in
the fifth column differ only in fine detail, a lock of hair or the shape of the smile. The attribute the
guidance reads is the direction between the two sentences, and that direction hardly moves with the
wording, so an operator need not search for the right words.
Stable Diffusion (Figure 11).
Six seeds each for A face of a doctor and A face of a firefighter,
unguided and under Debias Anything at a gender target of one half. The unguided model draws five
men and one woman for the doctor and six men for the firefighter, the skew of Table 3. Guidance
turns two doctor columns and three firefighter columns into women, reaching three of six in each, and
takes the images whose logit was already closest: the woman of the sixth doctor column is left alone.
White coats and stethoscopes, helmets and turnout gear, the framing and the expression of each seed
stay; only the face changes, and with it hair and, on two firefighters, a hood under the helmet. These
are the samples behind the gender row of Table 3.

FULL COMPARISON UNDER THE DIFFLENS PROTOCOL
Table 16 completes Table 3 with the two CLIP scores of Section B.5, and the last paragraph of this
section traces our residual discrepancy to its sources. CLIP-T lies within 0.01 of the unguided model
for every method on every attribute, so none of them, ours included, trades prompt adherence for
the attribute. CLIP-I separates the methods into two groups: the label-located methods (Shi et al.,
2025; Zaleska et al., 2026) and fine-tuning (Shen et al., 2024) keep 0.85 to 0.92 of the original, the
latent-space editors (Kwon et al., 2023; Li et al., 2024) 0.81 to 0.88, and ours sits at 0.84 to 0.85,
the level of the h-space distribution guidance (Parihar et al., 2024). Race is the attribute on which
DiffLens and ICM keep the most of the original image, above 0.90, and also the one on which they
move the discrepancy least.
Where the residual discrepancy comes from.
The FD of Table 3 is an average over the four
occupations of the protocol, and Table 15 gives the four terms. On gender they are far apart: doctors
reach 0.021 and firefighters 0.104, nurses stay at 0.296 and receptionists at 0.378. For a binary
attribute FD =

unguided model explains the split. Seshadri et al. (2024) measured the share of women that Stable
Diffusion 1.4 draws for each occupation under four prompt templates: 100% for nurse under every
template and 99.8 to 100% for receptionist, against 1 to 16% for firefighter and 4 to 58% for doctor.
These shares account for the unguided row of Table 3: an all-female or all-male batch scores 0.71,
and averaging the four templates gives per-occupation discrepancies of 0.71, 0.71, 0.63 and 0.32,
whose mean of 0.59 is close to the 0.56 we measure on Stable Diffusion 1.5 with the prompt of
the protocol. Reaching one half means creating male nurses and receptionists that the model never
draws on its own, whereas female firefighters and doctors exist in its output already; the adapter,
trained on the model’s own images of these jobs, has seen just as few. Guidance moves nurses
and receptionists from all women to three quarters, and stops there. The other guidance method of
Table 3, the h-distribution guidance of Parihar et al. (2024), is trained on gender labels and stops at
0.222, above ours; the editing methods pass this point because they change what the model computes,
whereas guidance reweights what it draws.

the encoder moves towards young is most likely in its twenties, which the counter files under 20–59.

model can follow. On race the residual is even across occupations, 0.17 to 0.21, so it belongs to the
attribute and not to any job; the four sentences name the four groups the counter uses, and ours is the
