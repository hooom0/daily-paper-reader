Abstract. Reference-guided style transfer aims to preserve the seman-
tic structure of a content image while transferring the visual appear-
ance of a style reference. Recent diffusion-based methods achieve im-
pressive stylization quality by exploiting strong pretrained generative
priors. However, training-free approaches still face a difficult trade-off
among style fidelity, content preservation, and content leakage. Direct
style injection may unintentionally transfer semantic content from the
style image, while fixed guidance schedules often ignore the time- and
state-dependent nature of diffusion sampling. To address these limita-
tions, we propose TRACE, a training-free diffusion style transfer frame-
work with Time-adaptive Residual Attention Control and Content-Style
dEcomposition. TRACE first performs offline CLIP-based subspace anal-
ysis to separate content and style directions from paired data. During in-
ference, it removes content-related components from the style reference
and style-related components from the content reference to reduce leak-
age. It then injects style information through residual cross-attention
and applies uncertainty-aware guidance to adapt the guidance signal
at each denoising step. Experiments show that TRACE achieves a fa-
vorable trade-off between stylization and preservation. Compared with
optimal-control-based baselines, TRACE substantially improves style fi-
delity (+17.28 CSD and +34.10 SRA). While, compared with styliza-
tion methods, it better preserves content structure (+12.80 DINO, +5.52
CLIP-I, and -8.19 LPIPS) and reduces directional semantic leakage by
29.5% in DCL. Our code is publicly available at TRACE.

Introduction
Image style transfer aims to synthesize an image that preserves the semantic
structure of a content image while adopting the visual appearance of a style ref-
erence. Early neural style transfer methods demonstrated that deep features can

separate and recombine content and style representations [10], while arbitrary
style transfer methods improved efficiency through feature-statistics matching,
whitening-and-coloring transforms, attention matching, and transformer-based
fusion [8,17,20,22]. More recently, diffusion models have provided strong gener-
ative priors for high-fidelity image synthesis and editing [14,26,30], motivating
diffusion-based style transfer methods based on inversion, attention manipula-
tion, reference feature injection, or training-free guidance [6,19,33,37].
Despite this progress, training-free diffusion style transfer still faces a difficult
trade-off among style fidelity, content preservation, and content leakage suppres-
sion. A style reference image is not a pure style signal: it often contains colors,
textures, object identity, semantic content, and spatial layout. Directly injecting
such features may therefore transfer undesired content from the style reference
to the generated image, a problem observed in recent reference-guided stylization
and personalization methods [27,33]. In addition, strong attention replacement
or feature injection can improve stylization but may overwrite the original con-
tent trajectory and distort object structure [6]. Finally, most guidance strategies
still rely on fixed or heuristic schedules [2,15,34,35], despite the time-dependent
and state-dependent nature of diffusion sampling. Recent uncertainty-aware dif-
fusion studies show that uncertainty can identify unreliable denoising regions by
estimating generation confidence or score variance under perturbations [7, 18].
This motivates using uncertainty as a state-dependent signal to adapt guidance
along the reverse trajectory.
Motivated by these observations, we propose TRACE, a training-free frame-
work for diffusion-based style transfer. TRACE treats stylization as a controlled
denoising process instead of a static feature-transfer operation. First, TRACE
performs an offline CLIP-based content-style subspace decomposition from paired
content-style data. At inference time, the learned subspaces remove content-
related components from the style reference and style-related components from
the content reference, reducing reference-induced leakage before sampling. Sec-
ond, TRACE introduces residual attention control, which injects the cleaned
style signal as an additive residual rather than directly replacing attention fea-
tures. This preserves the content trajectory while allowing style cues to interact
with content geometry. Third, TRACE formulates style guidance as a time-
dependent control signal and adapts it according to the uncertainty of the cur-
rent denoising state, strengthening corrective guidance in unstable states while
avoiding excessive perturbation in stable ones. Our contributions are summa-
rized as follows:
– We propose a CLIP-based content-style decomposition mechanism that re-
duces reference-induced content leakage before diffusion sampling.
– A residual style injection is introduced to control the injection of style infor-
mation additively without directly overwriting the content generation tra-
jectory.
– We also formulate training-free diffusion style transfer as a time-dependent
control problem and develop an uncertainty-aware adaptive guidance strat-
egy for state-dependent stylization control.

Related Work
2.1
Training-free Diffusion Style Transfer
Diffusion models have become powerful generative priors for image synthesis and
editing through iterative denoising [14,30,31], while Latent Diffusion Models im-
prove efficiency by operating in a compressed latent space with flexible attention-
based conditioning [26]. These advances have motivated diffusion-based style
transfer methods that exploit pretrained diffusion models. InST [37] learns a
style representation from a single painting through inversion, and DiffuseIT [19]
performs image-guided translation with disentangled content and style repre-
sentations. More recent training-free methods avoid fine-tuning by manipulating
attention or reference features. StyleID [6] injects style information through self-
attention feature manipulation, while InstantStyle [33] emphasizes the need to
decouple style and content information in feature space.
Despite their effectiveness, training-free reference-guided methods still face
a difficult trade-off between stylization strength and content preservation. A
style image often contains not only colors, textures, and artistic patterns, but
also object identity, spatial layout, and scene semantics. Directly injecting such
entangled features may cause content leakage, where undesired semantic struc-
tures from the style reference appear in the output [27, 33]. Existing methods
reduce this issue through feature statistics, attention matching, or feature decou-
pling [16, 17, 19, 22, 33]. However, most training-free methods do not explicitly
remove content-related components from the style reference before sampling.
TRACE addresses this limitation by learning CLIP-based content and style
subspaces, which are used to clean both references before attention-based style
injection.
2.2
Attention Control and Adaptive Guidance
Attention layers provide an effective interface for controlling diffusion genera-
tion. Prompt-to-Prompt [12] shows that cross-attention maps are closely related
to spatial layout, and subsequent works exploit attention features for editing,
personalization, and style transfer [6,21,32]. However, direct attention replace-
ment or key-value injection can overwrite the content trajectory, especially when
the style reference contains strong semantic structures. This motivates residual
attention control, where style information is injected additively while preserving
the original content generation path.
Guidance is another important mechanism for steering diffusion sampling.
Classifier-free guidance [15] modifies the denoising trajectory to improve condi-
tion adherence, while training-free guidance methods use external objectives or

energy functions without updating model weights [2, 5, 34, 35]. However, most
guidance strategies rely on fixed scales or heuristic schedules, which do not fully
account for the time-dependent and state-dependent nature of reverse diffusion.
Recent works interpret diffusion sampling as trajectory optimization or stochas-
tic optimal control [1, 27], suggesting that guidance should be treated as a dy-
namic control signal. Motivated by this view, TRACE introduces uncertainty-
aware time-dependent guidance that adapts the control strength according to
the stability of each denoising state.

4
Methodology
4.1
Overview
Given a content image Ic and a style reference Is, TRACE generates a stylized
image Ics in a training-free manner by reusing a pretrained diffusion model
(StableCascade [23]) without updating its parameters. TRACE first performs
an offline CLIP-based content-style decomposition, where paired content-style
embeddings are used to estimate a content subspace Qcontent and a style subspace
Qstyle via Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) [11].
During inference, these subspaces are used to remove style-related compo-
nents from the content representation and content-related components from the
style representation, yielding cleaned embeddings that reduce reference-induced
leakage. The cleaned style signal is then injected through residual attention, pre-
serving the content generation trajectory while transferring style information.
Finally, TRACE applies uncertainty-aware adaptive guidance at each denoising
step, where the style guidance direction gt is modulated by the denoising uncer-
tainty Σt to provide stronger correction in unstable states and more conservative
updates in reliable states.

Experimental Setups
5.1
Baselines
TRACE is compared with two groups of representative training-free diffusion
baselines. Stylization methods, including StyleID [6], AttenST [16], DiffuseIT
[19], and InstantStyle [33], mainly focus on transferring visual style through fea-
ture, attention, or reference-guided manipulation. OC-based methods, including
RB-Modulation [27] and ITOC [4], emphasize controlled reverse diffusion and
source preservation. Detailed descriptions of all methods and implementation
settings of TRACE are provided in Appendix D.1 and D.2, respectively.

D.K Le et al.
5.2
Dataset
For inference-time style transfer evaluation, we use 15 style reference images from
the StyleAligned benchmark [13] and 13 content images from DreamBooth [28].
For the offline content-style decomposition stage, we use the paired content-style
data from RB-Modulation [27]. Detailed statistics and analysis of the decompo-
sition dataset are provided in Appendix C.1.
5.3
Evaluation Metrics
We evaluate TRACE from three aspects: style fidelity, content preservation,
and content leakage.
– Style fidelity is measured by CSD style similarity (CSD) [29] and Style Re-
trieval Accuracy (SRA).
– Content preservation is measured by CLIP image similarity (CLIP-I) [25],
DINO image similarity (DINO) [3], and Learned Perceptual Image Patch
Similarity (LPIPS) [36].
– Content leakage is measured by Directional Content Leakage (DCL).
Detailed metric definitions are provided in Appendix D.3.

6.1
Qualitative Results
Figure 2 shows qualitative comparisons between TRACE and existing training-
free baselines. OC-based methods such as ITOC and RB-Modulation generally
preserve the content structure and produce visually stable images, but their
stylization strength is relatively weak, especially when the target style requires
more than simple color transfer. In contrast, stylization-oriented methods such
as AttenST, StyleID, InstantStyle, and DiffuseIT often produce stronger style
effects, but they may distort the object structure or introduce semantic content
from the style reference. This is mainly because strong attention manipulation
or AdaIN can act as a direct information transfer between the content and style
images, which improves style fidelity but also increases the risk of content leak-
age. TRACE achieves a more balanced result. It preserves the main object layout
and spatial structure while injecting visible style patterns into the generated im-
age. The advantage is particularly clear for styles that involve structural changes
(samples 3, 4) rather than only color shifts (samples 1, 2). These results demon-
strate that the proposed content-style decomposition, residual style injection,
and uncertainty-time adaptive guidance jointly improve stylization quality with-
out severely damaging the content structure. Additional qualitative comparisons
and diverse stylization results are provided in Appendix E.8.

7.3
Effect of Time-adaptive Guidance
To study the effect of guidance strength, we compare E0 with E4-E6. From
no adaptive guidance in E4 to constant guidance with λ = 1 in E5, stronger
guidance improves stylization, indicating that an appropriate control signal is
necessary for transferring style effectively. However, further increasing the fixed
guidance scale to λ = 2 in E6 leads to overly aggressive stylization, which weak-
ens content preservation and increases leakage. This trend shows that a fixed
guidance value is difficult to balance across different denoising states. In contrast,
TRACE uses uncertainty-time adaptive guidance to adjust the control strength
along the reverse trajectory, achieving a better balance between style fidelity
and content preservation. Additional analyses of timestep-wise uncertainty and
adaptive guidance are provided in Appendices E.2 and E.5.
7.4
Effect of Noise-level Scheduling
To study the effect of the starting noise level, we compare different values of ρ.
Instead of starting from pure noise, TRACE starts sampling from a fixed inter-
mediate noising step, which helps preserve the pose and spatial structure of the
content image. When ρ = 1.0, the trajectory starts from a highly noisy state,
it allows stronger stylization but causes noticeable content distortion and leak-
age. When ρ = 0.5, more content information is retained, leading to better pose
preservation but weaker style transfer. We therefore use ρ = 0.7 in TRACE,
which provides a better balance between preserving the content pose and en-
abling sufficient stylization. Additional visual results under different noise levels
are provided in Appendix E.3.
8
Conclusion
In this paper, we presented TRACE, a training-free diffusion framework for
reference-guided style transfer. TRACE integrates content-style decomposition,
residual style injection, and uncertainty-time adaptive guidance to balance style
fidelity, content preservation, and leakage suppression. Experiments show that
TRACE better preserves content structure and reduces semantic leakage com-
pared with stylization methods while achieving stronger stylization than OC-
based baselines. However, TRACE still has limitations when the style reference
contains strong structural patterns or shape-dominant elements. In such cases,
the transferred style may affect the pose, contour, or fine geometry of the content
object, leading to imperfect shape preservation. In future work, we will explore
more explicit structure- and pose-preserving mechanisms to further protect the
spatial layout of the content image during style injection.

C.1
Construction of Paired Content-Style Dataset
Fig. 4: Representative paired content-style samples used for decomposition analysis.
Rows show content identities and columns show style references.
To estimate the offline content and style subspaces, we construct a paired content-
style dataset from 350 content images and 100 style images. Instead of exhaus-
tively combining all content and style references, we randomly sample 10 style
images for each content image, producing 3,500 stylized pairs in total. Each gen-
erated pair is associated with a content index and a style index, allowing us
to analyze content-specific and style-specific variations in the CLIP embedding
space. We split the 3,500 paired samples into 3,150 pairs for offline subspace
estimation and 350 held-out pairs for decomposition analysis and validation. No
parameters of the pretrained diffusion model or the CLIP image encoder are
updated during this process. Moreover, the paired data used for subspace con-
struction and decomposition analysis is kept separate from the inference-time
evaluation pairs. Therefore, TRACE remains training-free with respect to the
diffusion backbone and uses the offline dataset only to estimate reusable content
and style directions.
The content set covers common objects, animals, people, vehicles, buildings,
landscapes, food, and fantasy objects. The style set covers illustration, painting,
3D rendering, logo, poster, photography, abstract art, and printmaking styles.

E.1
Residual Style Injection vs. Original Attention
(a) Residual style injection
(b) Original attention
Fig. 5: Comparison between residual style injection and original attention.
As shown in Fig. 5, the original attention strategy combines multiple attention
outputs by simple averaging. Specifically, the outputs obtained from base-content


TRACE: Time-Adaptive Attention Control for Style Transfer
29
attention, style-content attention, and content-only attention are averaged to
produce the final representation. Although this strategy mixes style and content
information, it treats all branches equally and may dilute the style signal or
disturb the content trajectory.
In contrast, the residual style injection used in TRACE keeps the base-
content attention output as the main trajectory and injects style only through
an additive residual. The residual is computed by subtracting the content-only
attention output from the style-content attention output and then adding the
scaled difference to the base-content representation. This way allows us to pre-
serve the original content trajectory while injecting only the style-related residual
information.
