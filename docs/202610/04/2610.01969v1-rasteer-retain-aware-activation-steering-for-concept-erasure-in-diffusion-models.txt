RASTEER:
RETAIN-AWARE ACTIVATION STEERING
FOR CONCEPT ERASURE IN DIFFUSION MODELS

ABSTRACT
Concept erasure aims to remove a target concept, such as a copyrighted style, a
recognizable character, or unsafe content, from a pretrained text-to-image diffu-
sion model while preserving its ability to generate other content. Existing acti-
vation steering methods build an erasure direction mainly from the target concept
and adjust model activations along it at inference time. However, target and re-
tained concepts often overlap in the model’s representation space, so this direction
also contains shared components that retained concepts rely on. Steering directly
along this direction can therefore suppress retained concepts and harm the gen-
eration of non-target content. To address this issue, we propose Retain-aware
Activation Steering (RASteer), a training-free method. RASteer first builds a re-
tain subspace from the concepts to preserve. Retain-Orthogonal Steering (ROS)
then removes components aligned with this subspace from the erasure direction,
making steering more specific to the target. Since fully removing the shared com-
ponents can weaken erasure, we further introduce Overlap-Adaptive Calibration
(OAC). At each layer and denoising step, OAC uses the overlap between the era-
sure direction and the retain subspace to control how much of each shared compo-
nent is removed, balancing target erasure and concept preservation. Experiments
on unsafe-content, instance, and artistic-style erasure across multiple backbones
and benchmarks show that RASteer matches or outperforms the activation steer-
ing and weight editing baselines we evaluate, achieving a better balance between
erasure and preservation.1

INTRODUCTION
Text-to-image (T2I) diffusion models (Rombach et al., 2022) are trained on web-scale data and
inevitably learn content that their providers cannot include in their products, such as copyrighted
artistic styles, recognizable characters, and unsafe images. When takedown requests, changes in
regulations, or safety audits require removing such content, retraining on filtered data is too costly,
while prompt- or output-level filters are easily bypassed (Rando et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2026;
Sarwar et al., 2026). Concept erasure offers a practical alternative: it directly edits a released model
to prevent it from generating a target concept (Gandikota et al., 2023; Kumari et al., 2023; Gandikota
et al., 2024; Gong et al., 2024; Lu et al., 2024; Li et al., 2026), and has become a key step in deploying
T2I models responsibly. Such edits must erase the target while preserving all other content, including
unrelated styles and characters, as well as open-domain generation.
Existing erasure methods broadly fall into two categories: weight editing and activation steering.
Weight editing fine-tunes the model or applies closed-form updates to its cross-attention matri-
ces (Gandikota et al., 2023; Kumari et al., 2023; Gandikota et al., 2024; Gong et al., 2024; Lu
et al., 2024; Li et al., 2026; Liu et al., 2025; 2026; Hu et al., 2026), writing these changes into model
parameters and increasing the cost of switching or undoing edits. By contrast, activation steer-
ing (Gaintseva et al., 2026; Huu-Tien et al., 2024; Xie et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2024b) keeps model
weights fixed and suppresses target concepts by adjusting intermediate activations during inference,

However, the target’s erasure direction may contain shared components that retained concepts also
rely on, causing collateral damage. In the top row of Figure 1a, steering along the original direction
to erase Sonic turns the retained character Snoopy into an ordinary dog. Figure 1b further shows
overlap between the alignment scores of target and retained activations: at the default threshold,
the gate incorrectly triggers on 65% of retained activations and misses 21% of target activations.
Raising the threshold reduces false triggers but misses more target activations. In the bottom row
of Figure 1a, Snoopy remains recognizable after full projection even though it is the erasure target,
showing that completely removing shared components can also weaken target erasure. These obser-
vations show the need to incorporate information about retained concepts into the erasure direction
to reduce collateral damage and improve target erasure.
To address these issues, we propose Retain-aware Activation Steering (RASteer), which uses infor-
mation about retained concepts to refine the erasure direction. It consists of two modules: Retain-
Orthogonal Steering (ROS) and Overlap-Adaptive Calibration (OAC). ROS constructs a retain sub-
space from the steering vectors of retained concepts. It identifies and reduces the shared components
of the original erasure direction that align with this subspace, making the direction more specific to
the target and reducing suppression of retained concepts. OAC adjusts the removal strength based on
the overlap between the erasure direction and the retain subspace at each layer and denoising step,
balancing target erasure and concept preservation. As shown in Figure 1c, RASteer further lowers
target classification accuracy compared with full projection while maintaining similar accuracy on
retained concepts, illustrating how adaptive calibration improves target erasure.
We evaluate RASteer on unsafe-content, instance, and artistic-style erasure across multiple back-
bones and benchmarks, comparing against activation-steering and weight-editing baselines under
the same evaluation pipeline. The results show that RASteer effectively suppresses target concepts
while largely preserving retained concepts and general image quality, achieving a better trade-off
between erasure and preservation. Our contributions are:
• We propose RASteer, a closed-form, training-free method that erases concepts by steering cross-
attention activations while keeping model weights fixed and edits reversible and composable.
• We introduce ROS to remove retain-subspace components from erasure directions and OAC to ad-
just removal strength at each layer based on erasure–retain overlap, without learnable parameters.
• Across unsafe-content, instance, and artistic-style erasure on multiple backbones and benchmarks,
RASteer achieves the best erasure–preservation trade-off among the compared methods.

RELATED WORK
Model Editing for Concept Erasure. Fine-tuning methods suppress concepts by optimizing the
denoiser (Gandikota et al., 2023; Kumari et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2024a) or training lightweight
modules (Lyu et al., 2024; Lee et al., 2025; Huang et al., 2024). Closed-form methods directly update
cross-attention weights: TIME (Orgad et al., 2023) uses least-squares editing, UCE (Gandikota et al.,
2024) adds retention constraints, and RECE (Gong et al., 2024) iteratively erases embeddings that
regenerate the target. MACE (Lu et al., 2024) combines closed-form editing with LoRA fusion
for mass erasure (Hu et al., 2021). SPEED (Li et al., 2026) restricts updates to the null space of
preserved concepts, while CURE (Biswas et al., 2026) applies an energy-scaled spectral projection.
These methods encode edits in model weights or trained modules, so switching or combining edits
involves managing the corresponding parameters.
Inference-Time Steering. The most recent family leaves the weights untouched and edits the gen-
eration process at inference: Safe Latent Diffusion (Schramowski et al., 2023) steers the sampling
trajectory away from unsafe content through classifier-free guidance, SAFREE (Yoon et al., 2025)
projects prompt embeddings off the unsafe concept’s subspace in text-embedding space, SAeU-
ron (Cywi´nski & Deja, 2025) ablates sparse-autoencoder features of the target, and CASteer (Gaint-
seva et al., 2026) subtracts the target’s steering vector from cross-attention activations, keeping the
edit reversible and composable. All of them, however, model only the concept to remove: none
takes the concepts to keep as input, so a retained concept that shares directions with the target is
suppressed along with it. RASteer keeps this weight-free runtime and supplies the missing ingre-
dient: it builds a retain subspace from the concepts to preserve and steers only along the erasure
direction orthogonal to it, with layerwise strength set by overlap.
Sequential and Continual Unlearning. Continual learning studies how models acquire a sequence
of tasks or concepts while mitigating interference with previously learned knowledge, whereas on-
line learning emphasizes adaptation to a stream of incoming data or objectives (Liu et al., 2020;
2021a;b; 2023a;b; 2024; Li et al., 2019; 2020; 2021; Luo et al., 2023; Fischer et al., 2024; Zhang
et al., 2023). Sequential unlearning presents a complementary challenge: rather than continually
acquiring new knowledge, a deployed model must continually satisfy new forgetting requests while
retaining everything else (Meng et al., 2022a;b; Ma et al., 2025; Gu et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2024).
Repeated parameter editing can accumulate interference across requests, making preservation in-
creasingly difficult. Our setting shares this continual adaptation perspective, but differs in that RAS-
teer performs each edit at inference time without updating model parameters, allowing multiple
erasure constraints to be introduced, removed, or combined without parameter drift.

METHOD
Section 3.1 formalizes the concept-erasure setting and shows how we extract a steering direction
for a concept. Section 3.2 then presents RASteer and its two modules: Retain-Orthogonal Steering
(ROS), which applies this direction after projecting out its retain-aligned part, and Overlap-Adaptive
Calibration (OAC), which sets the projection strength directly from data.
3.1
PRELIMINARIES

Retain-Orthogonal Steering (ROS).
To erase the target without affecting the retained concepts,
we need the component of the erasure direction ˆf that is specific to the target and free of them.

EXPERIMENTS
4.1
SETUP
Backbones and Benchmarks. We use Stable Diffusion v1.4 (Rombach et al., 2022) as the main
backbone and evaluate cross-model generality on Stable Diffusion v1.5 and v2.1, plus SDXL (Rom-
bach et al., 2022; Podell et al., 2024). We evaluate unsafe content on I2P prompts (Schramowski
et al., 2023), instances and artistic styles with matched retain concepts, and general open-domain
generation on MS-COCO captions (Lin et al., 2014). We compare with SPM (Lyu et al., 2024),
SAeUron (Cywi´nski & Deja, 2025), ESD-x/u (Gandikota et al., 2023), UCE (Gandikota et al., 2024),
MACE (Lu et al., 2024), RECE (Gong et al., 2024), and CASteer (Gaintseva et al., 2026).
Implementation Details. RASteer is training-free and updates no weights. For each concept, we
precompute its steering vectors offline and reuse them at inference. We steer every cross-attention
layer with the OAC rule and no per-concept tuning. We use official code or released weights under
default settings for every baseline. Experiments use NVIDIA A40. Appendix A.1 provides the
complete implementation details.

CLIP accuracy and CLIP score (Radford et al., 2021) on the erasure target and on every retained
concept. Unsafe content is counted by the exposed body parts detected with NudeNet (Bedapudi,

4.2
MAIN RESULTS
We compare RASteer with all baselines on unsafe-content, instance, and artistic-style erasure. De-
tailed unsafe-content statistics are provided in Table 1. Across these settings, Tables 2 and 3 report
general-content preservation and instance/style erasure, respectively. Overall, the results indicate
that RASteer offers a favorable erasure–preservation balance: it reduces unsafe detections, produces
consistently low classification accuracy on the erased instance and style targets in Table 3, and keeps
retained and open-domain generations close to the unedited model. Additional results on different
backbones are provided in Appendix A.2.
Erasure of Unsafe Content. On I2P, RASteer reduces exposed-body-part detections from 612 to
13, compared with 24 for CASteer and 83 for RECE. Table 1 provides the breakdown by body-
part category. This setting is particularly challenging because the I2P prompts often invoke unsafe
content without naming it directly. Prompt-triggered methods such as SPM therefore erase very
little, while the weight-editing baselines reduce unsafe content but retain substantially more residual
detections. Together with the COCO results below, these counts show that RASteer improves unsafe-
content suppression while preserving general generation ability.
Preservation of General Content. Table 2 shows that the stronger erasure of RASteer does not
come from globally degrading the model. Its image–caption alignment remains essentially un-
changed from the unedited backbone, and its paired perceptual drift is lower than that of several
methods that meaningfully erase nudity, including CASteer. RECE is competitive in distribution-
level preservation but is markedly weaker on unsafe-content removal, while UCE’s stronger model-
wide drift indicates that its edit is less localized. SPM’s small drift should be interpreted together
with its limited erasure. Together, the I2P counts in Table 1 and the preservation metrics in Table 2
distinguish selective erasure from leaving both unsafe and benign generations unchanged.

ABLATION STUDY
Component Ablation. Table 4 uses the first block of Table 3, erasing Snoopy and Van Gogh while
treating the other concepts in their respective groups as retained concepts. All variants use the same
vector configuration. Removing ROS recovers raw activation steering. Its retained-concept FID
increases consistently, accompanied by lower recognition accuracy for several retained concepts,
showing that ROS is responsible for limiting collateral changes. Removing OAC instead applies
the full retain projection at every layer and step. For both erased concepts, this variant has higher
target CLIP score and lower target FID, indicating that its outputs remain closer to the original
target semantics and distribution. Van Gogh accuracy is an exception to this trend, so we interpret
the erasure effect jointly through alignment and distributional change rather than accuracy alone.

unsafe-content removal. The default β = 2 on SD-v1.4 keeps retained-concept accuracy above 98%

CONCLUSION
We studied concept erasure through inference-time activation steering, where the target concept
may share substantial representation with concepts that should be retained. We traced the collateral
damage of raw steering to this overlap and proposed RASteer, a training-free method that leaves the
model weights unchanged. Its ROS module removes retain-aligned components from the erasure
direction, while OAC adjusts the removal strength according to the target–retain overlap. Across
multiple concept-erasure benchmarks and four Stable Diffusion backbones, RASteer balances target
suppression with preservation of retained concepts and general generation.
Limitations. First, our experiments focus on the Stable Diffusion family; extending RASteer to
newer architectures such as diffusion transformers and FLUX remains future work. Second, RAS-
teer relies on a predefined retain set, and poorly represented concepts may still be affected. Third,
when the target direction overlaps substantially with the retain subspace, the direction available for
erasure becomes limited, motivating adaptive retain-set construction or iterative steering.

Across SD-v1.4 and SD-v1.5, RASteer generally removes the target while producing structured
alternatives, whereas several baselines either retain recognizable target cues or alter nearby concepts.
SD-v2.1 is the more difficult regime: residual target cues occur more often, but the retained Mickey
Mouse and Hokusai rows remain comparatively stable under RASteer. On SDXL, target removal
is strong and the retained Pikachu and artistic-style rows expose the preservation advantage most
clearly; the remaining texture changes in erased-style rows also show that strong erasure is not
artifact-free. These recurring patterns match the quantitative erasure–preservation trade-off rather
than relying on a single favorable sample.
A backbone-specific reading further separates the failure modes. On SD-v1.4 and SD-v1.5, incom-
plete instance erasure often leaves a recognizable silhouette, while stronger interventions can replace
the entire subject or background. In the style rows, some methods suppress the named painter by
flattening the image into repetitive texture rather than preserving the prompt content. SD-v2.1 shows
the same effects more strongly: residual character shapes and dense repeated motifs coexist across
methods. The SDXL rows make collateral damage especially easy to see because its unedited out-
puts are highly consistent; the retained Pikachu poses and wave-like compositions stay close under
RASteer, whereas some baselines alter the subject, palette, or spatial structure. Thus the grid distin-
guishes successful semantic removal from mere visual degradation.
