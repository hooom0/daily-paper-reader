ABSTRACT
Diffusion models generate samples by learning to reverse a fixed corruption process,
and classifier-free guidance (CFG) is the standard mechanism for conditioning
this process on a desired class or prompt. CFG can be applied at varying guid-
ance strengths, and while higher strengths improve image quality and conditional
alignment, too high a guidance strength can degrade image quality and diversity.
Furthermore, CFG violates principled diffusion sampling dynamics, and existing
explanations for why it works despite the violation disagree on the underlying
theory or do not extend to deterministic samplers used in practice. We address
both these issues. We first frame CFG sampling as a continuous-time optimal
control problem, treating the sampling trajectory as a sequence of controls cho-
sen to maximise the probability of the desired condition. Solving the resulting
Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equation shows that CFG is recovered under specific
path costs when using an unconstrained control set. We argue this lack of constraint
is responsible for CFG’s failure at high guidance strengths, since it permits the
sampling path to move arbitrarily far from the current image estimate. To fix this,
we propose curvature-aware CFG (CACFG), which constrains the control set to a
hypersphere informed by the Gaussian regularisation used when training variational
autoencoders. We show that the control inputs produced by CFG sampling rou-
tinely violate this bound, and that across diffusion models, datasets, and guidance
schedules, CACFG achieves superior generative quality at mid-to-high guidance
strengths with a less severe quality-diversity tradeoff than regular CFG.

INTRODUCTION
Diffusion models generate samples by learning to iteratively reverse a fixed process that gradually
corrupts data with noise (Ho et al., 2020; Song et al., 2021b). Classifier-free guidance (CFG) (Ho &
Salimans, 2022) provides a mechanism to control the content of generated images by using a variable
guidance strength parameter to target a tilted conditional distribution. However, this tilted distribution

diversity and degrade image quality by introducing visual artefacts (Sadat et al., 2025; Karras et al.,
2024). Despite this, CFG is frequently used in practice, and practitioners select the largest guidance
strengths that do not harm image quality (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021; Ho & Salimans, 2022).
Previous work has proposed methods of fixing CFG artefacts: CFG++ (Chung et al., 2025) and
Interval-CFG (IntCFG) (Kynkäänniemi et al., 2024) each attribute CFG’s artefacts to the sampling
trajectory departing the image manifold, correcting for this by varying guidance strength across
the trajectory. A separate line of work asks why CFG works despite violating diffusion sampling
dynamics. Hypotheses attribute its effectiveness to a shared bias in the conditional and unconditional
denoisers (Karras et al., 2024), an implicit correction for the poorly-approximated tilted distribution
(Moufad et al., 2025), or an equivalence with a predictor-corrector scheme in the continuous-time
SDE limit (Bradley & Nakkiran, 2025). However, these explanations disagree on the mechanism
responsible, and they do not rigorously extend to commonly used deterministic samplers.
We address this gap by reformulating CFG sampling as a continuous-time optimal control problem:
rather than fixing the sampling trajectory as a function of stochastic transition kernels, we treat it as
a sequence of controls chosen to maximise the terminal reward p(ξ | x0), i.e., how probable it is
that the sample is of the desired condition/class ξ. Solving the resulting Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman
(HJB) equation shows that the optimal control recovers CFG when the control set is unconstrained
and the traversal penalty is expressed in the same scale as the diffusion training loss. To the best of
our knowledge, we are the first to frame CFG as a deterministic optimal control problem in this way.
Additionally, existing guidance scheduling methods such as CFG++ and IntCFG also emerge from
this same optimal control formulation under alternative choices of the traversal penalty (Appendix B).
By examining the path costs of this optimisation problem, we find that the CFG diffusion sampling
trajectory is encouraged to: align the conditional and CFG scores at early-to-mid sampling times;
converge towards a local maximum at late sampling times; and avoid traversing near a maximum too
early. This provides significant insight into how CFG works, and why it exhibits certain pathologies.
Our optimal control formulation shows that traditional CFG imposes no constraint on the control
set. This permits the sampling path to move arbitrarily far from the current image estimate given
sufficiently low traversal penalty. We argue this is directly responsible for CFG’s failure modes in
high-guidance regimes. To address this, we propose a constrained control set and corresponding
curvature-aware CFG scheme (CACFG) that relies on a property latent diffusion models have by
construction: the training objective of their variational autoencoder (VAE) is explicitly regularised
towards an isotropic Gaussian. We hypothesise that this forces most encodings to lie within a
hypersphere of an estimable radius, and we show empirically that the Tweedie estimates produced by
traditional CFG sampling frequently violate this theoretical upper bound, while those of CACFG do
not (Figure 1). We find that across diffusion models, datasets, and guidance schedules, CACFG has
superior generative quality at mid-to-high guidance strengths as measured by various quality metrics.
Our core contributions are as follows: (i) We show that diffusion guidance can be interpreted as a
continuous-time control problem; (ii) We show that classifier-free guidance (CFG) is one solution to
this control problem, and that existing guidance scheduling methods, such as CFG++ (Chung et al.,
2025) and IntCFG (Kynkäänniemi et al., 2024), arise from the same framework under alternative

RELATED WORK
Chung et al. (2025) attributes CFG artefacts such as colour oversaturation and broken invertibility to
an off-manifold effect: since ω > 1 extrapolates beyond the conditional and unconditional denoiser
outputs, the resulting noise estimate can fall off the data manifold. By reformulating guidance as
a manifold-constrained score-matching problem, Chung et al. (2025) appeal to the linear manifold
hypothesis (Chung et al., 2022) to compute a manifold constrained gradient to force the sample to
remain on the theorised linear image manifold. Alternatively, Interval-CFG (IntCFG) (Kynkäänniemi
et al., 2024) hypothesises that CFG artefacts can be attributed to when guidance is applied: guidance
at high noise levels collapses diversity by prematurely committing the trajectory to a small set of
modes, while guidance at low noise levels has minimal effect. Thus, IntCFG disables guidance
outside an interval of intermediate noise levels. Though motivated differently, both methods let the
guidance scale of (8) vary across the sampling trajectory, a fact we make concrete in Appendix B.
Alternatively, techniques such as Adaptive Projected Guidance (APG) (Sadat et al., 2025) reshape the
CFG update itself. However, none of this work provides a rigorous explanation as to why CFG is
able to generate conditional images, despite violating diffusion sampling dynamics.
Karras et al. (2024) attributes CFG’s effectiveness to a shared model bias: a capacity-limited denoiser
over-covers sparsely populated regions of the data manifold, and because the unconditional model
exhibits this bias more severely than the conditional one, their ratio acts as an attractive force that
pulls samples towards the image manifold. Moufad et al. (2025) show that the tilted distribution of
(6) can be recovered by augmenting CFG with a corrective Rényi-divergence term, and they argue
that CFG is just poorly approximating the true tilted distribution. Bradley & Nakkiran (2025) instead
prove that in the continuous-time SDE limit, the stochastic (DDPM) variant (Ho et al., 2020) of
CFG is identical to a predictor-corrector scheme that pairs DDIM (Song et al., 2021a) denoising
with a Langevin-dynamics corrector on the tilted distribution. However, this does not account for
deterministic (flow ODE) variants of CFG, and the explanations of Karras et al. (2024) and Moufad
et al. (2025) disagree on the key mechanisms.

REFRAMING CFG AS AN OPTIMAL CONTROL
We first consider the tilted density of (6), and note that raising p(ξ | xt) to any power ω > 0
leaves its maximiser (w.r.t. xt) unchanged. Thus, the CFG update of (8) can be viewed as naively
moving towards a local maximum of p(ξ | xt) at each timestep; however, this does not guarantee
maximisation with respect to the terminal state, x0. Treating CFG sampling as purely an optimisation
problem leads to the natural question: does CFG sampling construct a path that maximises p(ξ | x0)
subject to some reasonable path constraints? More concretely, we wish to understand whether CFG
constructs trajectories that terminate at a state, x0, that maximise p(ξ | x0) subject to interpretable
path costs that punish/reward movement from the initial position, xT .

following a fixed set of transition kernels, and we introduce a time-dependent control variable
ut ∈U(t) ⊆Rm taking values in a nonempty, closed, and convex time-varying control set U(t).

We now ask: what do the constraints of (16) imply about the paths constructed by CFG-like guidance?
The conditional score alignment term in (16) rewards paths that align the conditional and CFG scores,
which is reasonable, given we seek trajectories that converge to high-density conditional regions. This
term is scaled by g(s)2, which decreases as s →0 under most diffusion parameterisations (Karras
et al., 2022), suggesting that the conditional alignment matters most in early-to-mid sampling times.
The CFG score projection, Υ, can be thought of as the maximum allowable CFG movement given
the current Tweedie estimate, ˆx0(s, xs, ΩY ), at time s. Assuming that ˆx0 ∈U(s) (the control set
should contain all Tweedie estimates), then Υ is either 0 or aligned with the CFG score, ∇xsϕ, in
the sense that ⟨∇xsϕ(s, xs), Υ(s, xs)⟩≥0 (by Bertsekas, 2009, Prop. 1.1.9), and thus, the CFG
score regularisation term is ≤0. This term is negligible at early sampling times, αs →0 as s →T,
but grows as βs →0 as s →0, and the only way to avoid this penalty is to find a local optimum
(∇ϕ = 0) or enter a region that no longer has viable movement in the direction of the CFG score,

Thus, CFG-like guidance is an optimal control specified in the same loss scale as the denoising
training objective, and it finds paths that: (i) are encouraged to align the conditional and CFG scores
at early-to-mid sampling times; (ii) are encouraged to converge to a local maximum at late sampling
times; and (iii) are penalised for nearing local maximums at early sampling times.

CURVATURE-AWARE CFG (CACFG)
Section 4 shows that CFG-style guidance solves an optimal control problem, but says nothing about
whether the resulting control is a safe one to take. Compensating for weak class adherence with
a large guidance scale ω (or small r(s)) may move the sampling trajectory into regions with few
training samples, and thus, require the model to extrapolate. This is a known failure mode of neural
networks–adversarial regions populate extrapolated sections of the learned manifold, and they cause
networks to behave erratically (Szegedy et al., 2014; Goodfellow et al., 2015; Madry et al., 2018).

6
CACFG EXPERIMENTS
Diffusion models. We evaluate CACFG using two latent diffusion models of varying sizes: Stable
Diffusion 1.5 (SD1.5) (Rombach et al., 2022) and Stable Diffusion XL (SDXL) (Podell et al., 2024).
Both are text-conditioned latent diffusion models that run the diffusion of (1) on a VAE latent space
trained using the same objective of (9). SD1.5 and SDXL have differing generative resolutions
of 512×512 and 1024×1024, which have substantially different compute costs. Thus, for SD1.5
experiments we generate 10,000 samples for each guidance-dataset-strength setting, while for SDXL
we generate 2,000. We run both models on a NVIDIA RTX4090, and the curvature-aware projection
does not incur substantive compute overhead–all models run at their originally reported speeds.
Datasets. We generate images conditioned on two datasets: ImageNet (Russakovsky et al., 2015),
which provides class-conditional generation across 1000 object categories, and COCO (Lin et al.,
2014), which provides caption-conditioned generation–a more sophisticated generative task.
Guidance schedules. We compare three guidance-weight schedules ω(t): the constant-weight CFG
of (8) (Ho & Salimans, 2022), and the two scheduled alternatives CFG++ (Chung et al., 2025) and
IntCFG (Kynkäänniemi et al., 2024), whose exact forms are given in Appendix B. Each schedule
is evaluated both on its own and combined with the curvature-aware control set of (23), giving the
CACFG, CACFG++, and IntCACFG guidance schemes respectively. Across all curvature-aware
variants we fix the confidence level of (21) at p = 0.95, which corresponds to ru = 129.16 and
ru = 257.16 for SD1.5 and SDXL, respectively. Based on initial ablations (Appendix C.3), we use
the blending parameter γ = 0.5 and γ = 1.0 for SD1.5 and SDXL, respectively. Additionally, we
use the DDIM ODE solver (Song et al., 2021a) for SD1.5 and SDXL, as it is the most common
deterministic solver.
Guidance weights. We report the guidance weight of CFG and compute an approximate equivalent
weight under each other schedule. That is, we find schedule parameters, θ, such that

Metrics. We report nine metrics measuring image quality across varying axes. Fréchet Inception
Distance (FID) (Heusel et al., 2018) measures the similarity of real and generated image distributions.
Density and Coverage (Naeem et al., 2020) report the fidelity and diversity of the generated images
by comparing manifolds fitted to real and generated images. NIQE (Mittal et al., 2013), BRISQUE
(Mittal et al., 2012), and TReS (Golestaneh et al., 2022) are no-reference perceptual quality metrics,
scoring an image’s naturalness based on its pixel statistics. CLIP score (Radford et al., 2021) measures
prompt alignment via the cosine similarity between a generated image’s CLIP embedding and its
conditioning. Human Preference Score v2 (HPSv2) (Wu et al., 2023) is a model trained to mimic
human rankings of image quality. We note that HPSv2 and CLIP score are known to artificially
improve at large CFG strengths despite poorer image quality and increasing saturation (Xie et al.,
2026). We therefore additionally report Saturation (Sadat et al., 2025), computed as the mean of the
saturation channel after converting each image from RGB to HSV, to measure this effect.

improved or equivalent under the curvature-aware (CA) correction for all three guidance schedules
and both datasets, with the gap widening substantially at higher guidance strengths (Figures 2 and 6).
Since Density and Coverage improve alongside FID rather than trading off against it, the corrected
samples are more accurately contained within the real-image manifold and also cover a wider region
of it, indicating an improvement in both quality and diversity.

CONCLUSION
We show via the HJB equation that CFG and other guidance schedules can be derived as the optimal
control for a continuous-time optimisation problem. Existing CFG corrections assume the underlying
image manifold has some known geometry, but this is not guaranteed for latent diffusion models,
whose latent image manifold is governed by what the VAE has learned. We use the Gaussian
regularisation term of the VAE loss to infer that most image encodings lie within a hypersphere of
estimable radius. We then propose a curvature-aware CFG correction that constrains the control
set of our optimisation problem to a time-varying hypersphere whose radius is informed by the
estimated radius. We find that at high guidance scales this generates images with substantially better
quality, as measured by FID, NIQE, BRISQUE, TReS, Density, and Coverage. Thus, our constrained
guidance provides superior conditional generation with a less severe quality-diversity tradeoff than
that exhibited by unconstrained guidance methods, and functionally no additional compute cost.

A.1
LIMITATIONS
We note three limitations of our work, each of which we view as a promising direction for future work
rather than a fundamental shortcoming of the proposed optimal control framework. First, the control
set Uγ(t) we optimise over is time-varying but not state-varying; extending it to depend jointly on
sampling time and the current state xt would more directly incentivise training a model to perform
the image-manifold projection. Second, our optimal control perspective does not currently explain
guidance techniques such as APG (Sadat et al., 2025), which act by manipulating the guidance
gradient itself, rather than by reweighting it through the guidance schedule. Finally, our formulation is
posed for deterministic sampling processes, and does not immediately extend to stochastic samplers.
However, these extensions are unnecessary for understanding and improving traditional classifier-free
guidance on deterministic samplers, which was the primary objective of this work.
A.2
REPRODUCIBILITY
We ensure reproducibility by providing a detailed description of our algorithm in the main text (Algo-
rithm 1). Additionally, our code and the configuration files necessary to replicate our experiments are
made publicly available. Comparison methods are implemented using default parameters or with
parameters selected based on the respective authors’ tuning algorithms.
A.3
AI USE
We used large language models as a writing and verification aid during the preparation of this paper.
Specifically, AI assistance was used to check and typeset proofs that we had already derived by hand,
to edit and improve the clarity of rough prose drafts, and to assist with LaTeX formatting of figures.
AI assistance was not used to write experiment code, nor to generate the ideas, methods, or theoretical
contributions presented in this work; all such content originates from the authors.

specified in (26).

C.2
TRUE VAE LATENT DISTRIBUTION
Figure 5 shows the empirical distribution of SD1.5 VAE encodings (∥x0∥2) of the ImageNet validation
set, alongside the theoretical chi density implied by the isotropic Gaussian hypothesis proposed in
Section 5. The two do not agree because the VAE is trained with a regularisation term that encourages
encodings towards the theoretical density, but does not enforce their adherence. In practice we find
that the high-dimensional theoretical density appears as a narrow spike, whereas the true distribution
of encodings is visibly broader and right-skewed, with a tail extending up to an empirical maximum
of 211.19. Despite this, ru = 129.16 falls just past the mode of the empirical distribution, where the
bulk of the density has already accumulated, so the bound continues to capture a large proportion of
real encodings even though the theoretical density does not capture the distribution’s true shape.
Figure 5: Empirical distribution of SD1.5 VAE encodings (∥x0∥2) of the ImageNet validation set,
i.e. the distribution of ∥x0∥2. Red line denotes the theoretical chi density implied by the Gaussian
hypothesis in Section 5.
To test whether this mismatch matters in practice, we compare CACFG against CACFG*, an otherwise
identical scheme that instead sets ru = 211.19, i.e. the maximum SD1.5 VAE encoding magnitude
observed on the ImageNet validation set. From Table 1 we see that CACFG* is worse than CACFG
on BRISQUE, TReS, and Saturation at every guidance strength tested. FID, NIQE, Density, and
Coverage follow the same pattern from strength 7.5 onward. CLIP favours CACFG* at strengths 5
and 7.5, ties at strength 10, and favours CACFG at 15. HPSv2 favours CACFG* across all guidance
strengths. However, as we discuss in the main paper, Xie et al. (2026) tie HPSv2 to oversaturation,
and CACFG*’s saturation exceeds CACFG’s throughout, and all other metrics favour CACFG at
high guidance strengths. Thus, we interpret the higher HPSv2 of CACFG* as an instance of the
saturation-induced failure mode rather than a quality advantage.
We hypothesise that CACFG outperforms CACFG* because the tighter, theoretically-derived radius
encloses a denser region of the encoding manifold: Figure 5 shows valid encodings are more sparse
with distance from the origin, so the smaller radius keeps sampling trajectories within the well-
populated region the denoiser was trained on, rather than permitting convergence to low-confidence
regions (Szegedy et al., 2014; Goodfellow et al., 2015; Madry et al., 2018).
C.3
CURVATURE-AWARE UPPER BOUND BLENDING PARAMETER
The blending parameter γ interpolates between the two extremes of the Gaussian hypothesis underly-
ing Uγ(t): at γ = 0 the control set assumes the hypothesis holds exactly, while at γ = 1 it retains only
the raw upper bound ru (23). We sweep γ ∈{0.0, 0.1, . . . , 1.0} for CACFG on ImageNet, for both
SD1.5 (Table 2) and SDXL (Table 3). A consistent pattern emerges across both models: loosening
the control set steadily improves prompt alignment and human-preference scores (CLIP, HPSv2) and
steadily increases saturation, while the remaining fidelity metrics (FID, NIQE, BRISQUE, Density,
Coverage) improve sharply over the first few steps of the sweep and then plateau. No single value
of γ is simultaneously optimal across every metric, so selecting γ requires an explicit choice about
how to balance prompt alignment against the residual fidelity cost of moving away from γ = 0.0; we
describe that choice, and its cost, for each model below.

FID (14.97) is close to its sweep-best (14.53) and far below the γ = 0.0 value (16.95). Beyond

D.4
COMPARISON WITH ADAPTIVE PROJECTED GUIDANCE (APG)
Adaptive Projected Guidance (APG) (Sadat et al., 2025) decomposes the CFG update direction,
ˆx0(t, xt, ξ) −ˆx0(t, xt, ΩY ), into components parallel and orthogonal to the conditional prediction.
They show that manipulating the parallel and orthogonal gradient components allows the saturation
and image quality to be controlled more effectively. In this section we compare APG against CFG
and CACFG; however, unlike CFG++ (Chung et al., 2025) and IntCFG (Kynkäänniemi et al., 2024),
there is no form of ω(t) that converts the CFG-like guidance in Section 4 to APG. This prevents us
from comparing methods at equivalent path costs as we do in the main paper. To address this, we
instead compare methods across a range of strengths. We report image quality of samples generated
using CFG (Ho & Salimans, 2022), CACFG, and APG (Sadat et al., 2025) across a range of guidance
strengths for the ImageNet (Russakovsky et al., 2015) and COCO (Lin et al., 2014) datasets, and
SD1.5 (Rombach et al., 2022) and SDXL (Podell et al., 2024) diffusion models.
Counting only best-in-category and second-best-in-category results, we find CACFG is equal to
or better than all other guidance methods; however, this overlooks certain patterns of performance
(see Tables 6 and 7). We find APG has lower saturation across all regimes, which is reasonable
given that it was designed specifically to address oversaturation. By comparison, CACFG’s strengths
are more nuanced: it achieves superior no-reference quality (NIQE, BRISQUE) across models and
datasets. However, on SD1.5 it most consistently attains the best or second-best Density/Coverage
scores, whereas on SDXL, CACFG’s advantage shifts to TReS, with FID differences between all
three methods narrowing to within hundredths. This suggests CACFG’s benefit is not tied to a fixed
metric, but rather it maintains a strong performance across metrics as model scale changes. Taken
together, no method is necessarily favoured, but CACFG is more theoretically grounded due to its
optimal control formulation, and can be applied across guidance schedules, while APG requires
tuning to a specific diffusion model.
