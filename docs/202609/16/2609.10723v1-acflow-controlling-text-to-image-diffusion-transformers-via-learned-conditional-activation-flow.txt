ABSTRACT
Text-to-image diffusion transformers (DiTs) are powerful generators, yet direct
prompting provides limited control interface for style intensity and can fail to
suppress unwanted concepts. To enable these controls, we introduce ACFLOW,
an inference-time controller that transports intermediate layer image-token acti-
vations through a learned concept-conditioned velocity field while keeping the
base DiT frozen. A textual concept description specifies the desired intervention,
while the integration horizon provides a continuous control parameter. The field
produces token-varying, activation-dependent updates. With parameters shared
across concepts within each task family, the field supports fine-grained descrip-
tions and generalizes to concepts unseen during training without per-concept fit-
ting. On style control, ACFLOW achieves the best style–content trade-off among
the evaluated baselines in the high-style-alignment regime. At a fixed operat-
ing point, ACFLOW attains style–content alignment of 0.5365/0.2860, compared
with 0.4397/0.2684 for the baseline with the highest style alignment. Qualita-
tive results demonstrate suppression of diverse concepts, including cases where
direct prompting fails. Our analyses support the learned velocity field as an adap-
tive control mechanism, with update directions varying across tokens and depend
on their activation states. Our code is available at https://github.com/
Nove1yst/AcFlow.

INTRODUCTION
Text-to-image (T2I) diffusion models have become powerful tools for visual creation, yet their gen-
eration capabilities do not translate into precise user control. A model may readily generate a styl-
ized image without allowing users to smoothly adjust the degree of stylization, or depict a concept
without excluding it even when requested. Verbal modifier such as ’weak’ or ’strong’ do not provide
a calibrated control axis, producing similarly pronounce level of stylization; appending instructions
such as ’no lighthouse’ can leave the unwanted concept visibly present. Such control matters when
users want to explore different visual treatments of the same content or suppress unwanted concepts
from appearing, but directly prompting the generative model falls short in these cases.
Existing control mechanisms offers limited flexibility for these requirements. Parameter-level adap-
tation can provide a control interface, but methods based on concept-specific optimization require
additional fitting for each new concept (Gandikota et al., 2024a), affect non-target concepts (Lyu
et al., 2024) or risk degradations of previously learned control capabilities during sequential mod-
ification (Smith et al., 2023). This motivates an inference-time controller that shares parameters
across concepts, acts upon a user-specified concept with an explicit parameter for controlling the
intervention strength, while the base generator parameters keep frozen.
To this end, we introduce ACFLOW, which controls frozen DiTs by transporting intermediate layer
activations through a learned concept-conditioned velocity field at inference time. A textual concept
description specifies the desired intervention, and the integration horizon T provides a continuous

Our main contributions are:
• Control as a flow field. We formulate inference time control as transport through a learned
velocity field in activation space. Image-token activations move through the field, receiving
token-varying, activation-dependent updates.
• Concept-shared control and generalization. We condition the field on textual concept
descriptions, training one field across concepts within a task family without per-concept
fitting. The shared field generalizes to unseen concepts during training and responds to
fine-grained descriptions beyond coarse concept labels.
• Continuous style control and concept suppression. ACFLOW enables progressive style
modulation with the flow horizon as a continuous control parameter, achieving the best
style–content trade-off among the evaluated methods in the high-style-alignment regime.
Qualitative concept suppression results further demonstrate the applicability where direct
prompting leaves unwanted concepts present.
• Probing the learned field. Controlled experiments demonstrate the value of token-specific
updates, while trajectory analysis reveals that the field adapts its direction and energy al-
location systematically through denoising steps. These findings support the value of an
adaptive activation transport field beyond a fixed concept direction broadcast across tokens.

RELATED WORKS
Continuous concept control. Attribute Control (Baumann et al., 2025) identifies concept-specific
directions in text embeddings through optimization, enabling continuous modulation. Concept Slid-
ers (Gandikota et al., 2024a) learns concept-specific low-rank adapters (Hu et al., 2021) in the dif-
fusion backbone and continuously adjusts their influence through an inference-time scale. Text
Slider (Chiu et al., 2026) learns such adapters in the text encoder, enabling continuous attribute con-
trol. All approaches fit adapters for individual target concepts. ACFLOW instead trains a shared, text-
conditioned field within each task family and uses the flow horizon to adjust intervention strength,
including for concepts unseen during training.
Activation intervention for T2Is. Recent methods have proposed to control generation by mod-
ifying a model’s intermediate representations at inference time. ICM (Zaleska et al., 2026) uses
linear probes to identify fixed attribute directions, while Semantic Steering (Li et al., 2026) con-
structs a fixed target-to-safe direction. CASteer (Gaintseva et al., 2026) and SHIFT (Konovalova
et al., 2026) retain concept-specific directions but dynamically vary their magnitudes. DSAS (Fer-
rando et al., 2025) learns activation-dependent scalar gates for a prescribed steering transformation.
PID Steering (Nguyen et al., 2026) uses feedback to adjust the magnitude of a mean direction. In a
parallel branch, AcT (Rodriguez et al., 2025) estimates coordinate-wise affine maps between source
and target activations. SteeringDiffusion (Wu & Summa, 2026) predicts condition-dependent affine
modulation for multiple styles. Dynamic scaling and affine maps extend fixed vectors while con-
straining updates to a prescribed operation family.
Nonlinear activation intervention for T2Is. Nonlinear intervention allows the update direction to
vary with the activation state. Conditioned Activation Transport (CAT) (Chrabaszcz et al., 2026)
learns a geometry-gated nonlinear displacement, but computes it from a mean-pooled activation
and broadcasts the same update across tokens. Flow matching (Lipman et al., 2022) provides a
continuous velocity-field formulation of transport; ACFLOW uses a learned concept-conditioned
field to transport image-token activations.

3.1
CONTROL AS A FLOW FIELD
Let Gθ denote the frozen denoising velocity predictor of a DiT-based (Peebles & Xie, 2023) T2I
model with hidden dimension d. For one sample, the output of a selected single-stream block b is a
residual-stream state h ∈RS×d; we omit the batch dimension throughout. Here S = Stxt + Simg
counts text and image tokens, and hi ∈Rd denotes token i. Given a source prompt p (written psrc in
training) and a concept description c, we seek an edited state h′ that induces the requested concept
change while preserving non-target content. For example, for style control, p is a content prompt
and c describes the style of the image (e.g., in pop art style, use reflective layers).
At a fixed denoising noise level σ ∈[0, 1], we define an activation flow field φt(·; c, σ) : RS×d →
RS×d, parameterized by the field weights ϕ. For brevity, we write φt(h) with c, σ, and ϕ implicit.

EXPERIMENTS
We test the capability of ACFLOW on two control tasks: continuous style control and concept sup-
pression, both not achievable by directly prompting DiTs. Unless stated otherwise, experiments use
frozen FLUX.1-dev at 512 × 512 resolution, 28 denoising steps, guidance 3.5, single-stream block
b = 16, train with T = 1 and N = 3. NSFW suppression is in App. B; Z-Image (Cai et al., 2025)
portability is in App. A.
4.1
CONTINUOUS STYLE CONTROL
Fine-grained style conditions. MegaStyle (Gao et al., 2026) specifies each image’s style through
a fine-grained natural-language description. For our experiments, we group these descriptions into
style families and use this grouping to construct family-disjoint training and evaluation splits. We
train one conditional field across the training families. At generation time, the frozen generator
receives the content prompt and the field receives the style description. Style alignment is the
reference-image cosine similarity from the released MegaStyle encoder; content alignment is the
CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) similarity to the content prompt.
Baselines. We compare activation intervention methods: Linear-AcT (Rodriguez et al., 2025) at its
original locations and at our image-token site, the corresponding Mean-AcT variants, a diffusion
adaptation of Activation Addition (Turner et al., 2024), SHIFT (Konovalova et al., 2026) and pa-
rameter adaptation methods: Concept Sliders (Gandikota et al., 2024a) and Text Slider (Chiu et al.,
2026). To provide a setting for fair comparison, for each held-in style families, we fix a style descrip-
tion as each baseline requires fitting for each specific style, while ACFLOW supports fine-grained
style description and is shared across conditions. Implementation details are in App. C.3.

These results show that the field adapts its update geometry across noise levels along the generation
trajectory: both direction structure and the distribution of energy across tokens change systemat-

∆(σ) = a(σ)∆ref would leave both the off-axis energy fraction and the top-token energy share
unchanged for nonzero a(σ), unlike the observed curves.

CONCLUSIONS AND LIMITATIONS
We introduced ACFLOW, a concept-conditioned velocity field that steers image-token activations
at a single site in a frozen DiT. Its token-varying, activation-dependent updates share learned pa-
rameters across concepts, enabling fine-grained descriptions and generalization to held-out concepts
without per-concept fitting. The integration horizon provides continuous control across operating
points. Our experiments demonstrate strong style–content trade-offs and show the applicability to
concept suppression. The limitation is that ACFLOW does not guarantee preservation of the original

image composition. Intervention can change object positions, poses, and scene layout alongside the
intended concept. Specific concept control remains an open challenge.

ADDITIONAL APPLICATION TO NSFW SUPPRESSION
To further apply ACFLOW to more applications, we test whether ACFLOW can suppress an unsafe
concept without destroying the remaining prompt content.
Setup. We use FLUX.1-dev at 28 denoising steps, 512 × 512 resolution and guidance 3.5 as the
frozen DiT. We train one field on 100 unique unsafe–clean prompt pairs from the Nudity category of
Six-CD (Ren et al., 2025), using ten target rollouts per pair. Every sample uses the same condition
erase nudity. We train for 6,000 updates. Strength s = 2 is the default operating point selected
for its erasure–preservation trade-off; s = 3 is included as a stronger maximum-suppression setting.
Evaluation uses the 931 I2P prompts whose original category includes sexual content (Schramowski
et al., 2023). We count detections over nine exposed-body-part classes with NudeNet (Bedapudi,
2019) and measure perceptual quality with MUSIQ (Ke et al., 2021) and similarity to a same-prompt,
same-seed unedited FLUX image with DINO Image-to-Image similarity (DINO-I2I) (Oquab et al.,
2023) and LPIPS (Zhang et al., 2018).
Direct-prompt results. ACFLOW reduces the NudeNet count from 402 to 73 at s = 2 and to 38
at s = 3. At the default s = 2 point it removes substantially more detections than the strongest
external baselines in this local comparison, while attaining higher DINO-I2I and lower LPIPS than
CA, ESD-x, and negative prompting. Increasing the strength to s = 3 yields a further 47.9%
reduction relative to s = 2, but also lowers DINO similarity from 0.612 to 0.534 and raises LPIPS
from 0.509 to 0.563. MUSIQ remains above the unedited FLUX value at both strengths. These
results show that the additional suppression comes primarily with content drift, instead of a collapse
in visual quality. Among the three strongest external baselines by NudeNet total (CA, ESD-x, and
NP), ACFLOW at s = 2 achieves both the highest DINO-I2I similarity and the lowest LPIPS with
removing substantially stronger suppression capability.
Adversarial prompt attacks. Table 5 shows that, in the aligned Ring-A-Bell suite, ACFLOW has
the lowest clean ASR (9.17%) and the lowest attacked ASR (25.7%) for the primary attack and
43.1% for the three-configuration Union.

IMPLEMENTATION AND REPRODUCTION DETAILS
This appendix records the local training and evaluation protocols for the main style experiments
and supplementary suppression benchmarks. Unless noted otherwise, all methods use FLUX.1-dev,
512×512 output, 28 denoising steps, embedded guidance 3.5, and the same prompt and seed for
every paired comparison. The base-model snapshot is held fixed across methods. For ACFLOW,
the frozen FLUX parameters are never updated; all learned parameters belong to the activation-flow
module.
C.1
BACKBONE-SPECIFIC IMPLEMENTATION
FLUX.
T5 (Raffel et al., 2020) concept tokens are projected to the activation width, and the sep-
arate pooled CLIP feature supplies global conditioning. Cross-attention is modulated by flow time
t; the subsequent native-style self-attention/MLP parallel block is modulated by denoising noise
level σ. The output residual is the velocity, with the Euler step size T/N applied separately. The
generator and text encoders remain frozen; the field, including its concept projection, is trained.
15


Preprint
Z-Image.
The unified sequence is ordered as image tokens followed by text tokens; we update
only the image slice, after layer 19 for style and layer 25 for concept suppression. Qwen3 (Yang
et al., 2025) penultimate hidden states pass through the native caption embedding and two context-
refiner blocks, including padding, masks and rotary position conventions, before supplying concept
K/V. The field preserves Z-Image’s RMSNorm, QK normalization, gated attention and SwiGLU
branches. Cross-attention uses flow time t, while self-attention and the FFN use native model time
1 −σ. There is no pooled concept vector. The concept encoder modules copy weights from the
original caption modules and are frozen. In the concept suppression run, self-attention and FFN are
initialized from the hooked backbone layer, with initial gate bias 0.1. Training and inference apply
ACFLOW only to the positive CFG branch.
C.2
TRAINING BUDGETS AND HYPERPARAMETERS
The FLUX runs have a 20k-update budget, effective batch size 16, learning rate 3 ∗10−4 warmup
followed by linear learning-rate decay, gradient clipping 1.0, and seed 42. The Z-Image runs have
a 10k-update budget with other parameters the same as the FLUX runs. Checkpoint selection uses
the fixed 128-prompt held-in development set. Training uses T = 1.
All displayed generation protocols use 512×512 images and 28 denoising steps. FLUX uses embed-
ded guidance 3.5; Z-Image uses CFG 3.5 with scheduler shift 6. The Z-Image style and suppressions
run have a configured 10k budget. HQ-Edit suppression trains on 2,000 removal pairs, samples noise
levels logit-normally (mean 0, standard deviation 1). Its 256-case test split is target-disjoint.
C.3
CONTINUOUS STYLE CONTROL
Training.
We group MegaStyle’s per-image style descriptions into families to define our experi-
mental partition. The training split contains 39 families with 512 style-content prompt pairs, each
with its own fine-grained style description; the six held-out families are classical portraiture, dark
fantasy, geometric abstraction, pre-Raphaelite, retro-futurism, and woodcut print. The target latent
is a VAE encoding of the real MegaStyle image. Source and target velocity predictions share the
same noised latent, with the content-only source prompt and content-plus-style teacher prompt.
Style metric.
The style metric is the image similarity between the style-reference image and the
evaluated image, both encoded with a fine-tuned style-sensitive SigLIP (Zhai et al., 2023) encoder
provided by MegaStyle. The reference image is provided alongside the style-content pairs as the
style ground truth.
Aligned-style baseline protocol.
The eight fixed families are cubism, baroque, art nouveau, ink
wash, glitch art, art deco, paper art, and psychedelic, selected before the baseline runs with two
families from each quartile of the historical direct-teacher style means. For each family, one de-
scription/reference pair was frozen by filtering for typical teacher style and text scores. The eight
fixed families are crossed with 32 independent COCO-2017 (Lin et al., 2014) validation captions,
yielding 256 evaluation cases. All methods use the same descriptions, reference images, content
prompts, and initial noise. The fitting captions are COCO-2017 train captions, counted 256 per
style. This baseline calibration is separate from learning our shared 39-family field.
AcT.
Both variants of AcT follow the original implementation. Linear-AcT follows incremen-
tal forward-order fitting over all 19 multimodal blocks and the first 15 single-stream blocks at its
original sites. It fits both streams at the original locations. The location-matched variant fits only
block-16 image tokens. Mean-AcT uses mean shifts and has its own causal fitting pass at the original
sites. All maps are extracted at the highest-noise one-step response and reused at all 28 generation
steps; no timestep-specific maps are fitted. Each style has its own baseline fit.
ActAdd.
ActAdd uses only the first contrast pair at block 16. Each style has its own baseline fit.
SHIFT.
SHIFT uses the same training-caption pairs per style to fit first-response text-attention
directions and two probability SVMs at each of the 19 double-stream blocks, together with a
16


Preprint
pooled-CLIP mean shift. Directions are reused at all 28 denoising steps with the released classifier-
dependent scaling and activation-norm restoration. A common scale multiplies DiT strength 1000
and pooled-text strength 1.5.
Concept Sliders.
Concept Sliders follows the authors’ released FLUX extension. We train one
rank-16 attention adapter per style for 1,000 updates with batch size 1, learning rate 2 × 10−3,
and constant AdamW scheduling. Its xattn configuration fixes orthogonal up projections and
trains only the down projections, with LoRA alpha 1. We cycle the same 256 training captions.
Training retains the released velocity-contrast target with coefficient 2 and positive-prediction norm
restoration. The adapter is active at all 28 evaluation steps.
Text Slider.
We directly port the method of Text Slider (Chiu et al., 2026) to FLUX.1-dev, retain-
ing the authors’ released enhancement loss and text-encoder LoRA formulation. For each style, we
train rank-4, alpha-1 adapters on all CLIP attention query, key, value, and output projections for
500 updates, with batch size 1, AdamW at learning rate 2 × 10−4, and seed 6666, cycling the same
256 training captions. The positive prompt adds the frozen style description to the content caption;
the neutral, unconditional, and target prompts are the content caption alone. The original imple-
mentation trains separate CLIP and OpenCLIP branches; FLUX consumes only CLIP. We therefore
retain its hidden-state and pooled-output losses, each weighted by 768/2048 to preserve the CLIP
contribution to the original concatenated objective, and omit the unused OpenCLIP branch. At in-
ference, only pooled CLIP conditioning changes. We map the original delayed intervention to the
last 22 of 28 denoising steps, after a six-step delay. This is a direct method transplant with the stated
FLUX-dev interface adaptations.

C.4
CONCEPT SUPPRESSION
We construct removal training data from two instruction-based editing datasets: InstructPix2Pix for
FLUX and HQ-Edit for Z-Image. Each example provides a source caption containing the unwanted
concept and an edited caption specifying its removal. These serve as psrc and ptgt, respectively.
We generate the target latent z0 with the corresponding frozen backbone conditioned on ptgt; the
original datasets supply the text pairs only.
InstructPix2Pix removals (FLUX).
We mine removal pairs from InstructPix2Pix (Brooks et al.,
2023), retain clean pairs, and extract the removed concept from the caption difference, discarding
unresolved concepts or phrases. The field condition is erase <concept>. The training set
contains 1,158 pairs covering 1,122 concepts. The held-in set contains 149 pairs covering 91 training
concepts, with no source prompts shared with training. The held-out set contains 173 pairs covering
150 concepts absent from both training and held-in sets. Thus, the two evaluation splits test new
prompts for seen concepts and transfer to unseen concepts, respectively. Target latents are generated
with FLUX.1-dev using 20 denoising steps and guidance 3.5; the reported suppression evaluations
use 28 steps.
HQ-Edit removals (Z-Image).
We consider both forward and inverse edits in HQ-Edit (Hui et al.,
2024) and retain text-consistent, single-target removal instructions. Filtering rejects compound edits
and requires the extracted target phrase to occur in the source caption but not the edited caption.
The field receives the removal instruction itself as c. We select 2,000 training pairs and 256 test
pairs. Splitting groups removal targets by phrase containment, preventing related names such as

C.5
NSFW SUPPRESSION
Shared evaluation.
We use the 931 prompts whose original I2P category contains sexual and
the official per-row evaluation seed. Generation uses a maximum sequence length of 256. The
unedited control is regenerated with the same prompt and seed as every intervention. NudeNet uses
the official released checkpoint at confidence 0.6, and the headline count sums detections over the
nine exposed-body-part classes; a single image may contribute more than one detection. MUSIQ is
computed per generated image, while DINO-I2I and LPIPS compare each output with its matched
unedited-FLUX image.
ACFLOW.
The NSFW field is trained on 100 unique unsafe/clean Six-CD Nudity pairs, repeated
over ten rollout seeds to produce 1,000 training rows, all with condition erase nudity. It is a
T5- and sigma-conditioned AdaLN-dual field applied to image tokens at FLUX single-stream block
16, with three Euler integration steps and no presence gate. Optimization runs for 6,000 updates
with batch size 16, learning rate 10−3, 100 warmup steps followed by a constant schedule, gradient
clipping 1.0, and seed 42. Strengths 2 and 3 use the same checkpoint.
UCE.
UCE is reproduced with the released FLUX closed-form implementation editing nudity.
Both use erase scale 10, preserve scale 1, and λ = 0.1, producing a distinct edited weight state for
each setting.
EraseAnything.
We use the official released Flux-erase-dev nudity LoRA, without local
retraining. The rank-8 adapter acts on the text-side query/key projections in all 19 FLUX dual-
stream blocks.
Concept Ablation.
The CA row is a local FLUX port of the model-based recipe. We cycle the
204 non-empty prompts in the released people.txt bank to generate 1,000 base-FLUX anchor
images with seeds 42+i. The target nudity, nsfw is inserted using the three released templates.
Training uses moving-model target-to-anchor regression plus prior preservation for 400 optimizer
steps, effective batch size 8, scaled learning rate 6.4 × 10−5, and horizontal flips. The SD attention
parameter group is mapped to Q/K/V/output projections in all 19 FLUX dual-stream blocks; the
single-stream and non-attention parameters remain frozen.
MACE.
The MACE row is a local FLUX port of the released explicit-content recipe. Four lexical
targets, nudity, naked, erotic, and sexual, map to a person wearing clothes. For
each term we generate eight 512-pixel images at 30 steps and guidance 7.5, obtain Grounding-
DINO-base(Liu et al., 2024)/SAM-Huge masks (Kirillov et al., 2023), train a separate rank-1 text
K/V LoRA for 120 steps at 10−5, and apply MACE closed-form fusion. The released COCO-30K
prompts provide preservation constraints. The FLUX port maps CFR to context embedder and
the LoRAs to text-side K/V projections in all 19 dual blocks; a ridge equal to 0.1 times the mean
Gram diagonal stabilizes the near-singular T5 system.
ESD-x.
ESD-x follows the released FLUX trainer with erase concept nudity, on-policy syn-
thetic latents, 1,400 iterations, learning rate 10−4, batch size 1, 512-pixel resolution, and negative-
guidance code value 2.0. At the pinned implementation this code value is equivalent to the original
paper’s η = 1 target; it is fixed during training and is not an inference-time sweep.
Negative prompting.
NP is checkpoint-free and uses native FLUX true-CFG path. Both text
encoders receive nudity, nsfw; true cfg scale and the distinct embedded-guidance input
are both 3.5. No prompt or scale is selected on I2P.
SLD-Medium.
SLD is an inference-only local port to FLUX velocity predictions. It uses safety
concept nudity, nsfw in both encoders and the Medium preset: safety guidance 1000, warmup
10, threshold 0.01, momentum scale 0.3, and momentum beta 0.4. Positive, empty, and safety
